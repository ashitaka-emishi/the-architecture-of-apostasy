# Prophetic Warning Vs Omniscient Verdict

Use this note to keep the project's prophetic register urgent without pretending to know more than the evidence, Scripture, or human review can bear.

Prophetic warning is necessary. Omniscient verdict is forbidden.

## Prophetic Warning

Prophetic warning names a pattern of danger, calls for repentance, and exposes the visible shape of false worship.

It may speak urgently when the evidence warrants urgency. It may name apostasy, idolatry, false sacrifice, corrupted symbols, and fallen liturgies. It may warn that a person, church, institution, nation, or cultural order is becoming visibly conformed to another lord.

Prophetic warning should:

- identify the created good under threat;
- name the visible pattern of corruption;
- distinguish evidence from interpretation;
- call the reader toward repentance and discernment;
- preserve the possibility of Christological restoration;
- remain open to rival readings and correction where evidence is incomplete.

## Omniscient Verdict

Omniscient verdict speaks as if the project has final access to motives, hidden histories, divine judgment, or the settled spiritual state of persons and communities.

It turns warning into finality. It confuses urgency with certainty. It treats interpretation as if it were divine omniscience.

Omniscient verdict appears when a chapter:

- declares total apostasy without proportionate evidence;
- treats complex history as settled by a single theological sentence;
- assigns hidden motives without warrant;
- refuses rival readings as blindness or bad faith;
- uses prophetic force to avoid sourcing;
- diagnoses collapse without Christological restoration.

## Working Distinctions

| Question | Prophetic Warning | Omniscient Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| What does it claim? | This visible pattern requires repentance. | This person's or institution's final state is settled. |
| How does it use evidence? | Evidence supports a warning and invites testing. | Evidence becomes a pretext for final judgment. |
| How does it treat rivals? | Rival readings are considered and answered. | Rival readings are dismissed as corruption. |
| What does it seek? | Repentance, discernment, pruning, restoration. | Certainty, accusation, closure, superiority. |
| How does it end? | In Christological exposure and restoration. | In collapse, contempt, or spectacle. |

## Editorial Rule

When using prophetic language, identify the claim type and evidence level.

Acceptable:

- "This pattern resembles apostasy because..."
- "This institution shows markers of self-protective false worship..."
- "This reading is a typological warning rather than a final historical mapping..."

Avoid:

- "This proves the whole tradition is apostate."
- "No faithful reading is possible here."
- "Their motives are obviously..."
- "The judgment is final."

## Reviewer Questions

- Does the work distinguish warning from final verdict?
- Does it identify evidence, interpretation, and speculation?
- Does it include rival readings or cautions where needed?
- Does it avoid binding the reader's conscience beyond the evidence?
- Does it resolve in Christ rather than contempt?
