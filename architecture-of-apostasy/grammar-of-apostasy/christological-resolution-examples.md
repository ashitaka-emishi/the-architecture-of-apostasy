# Christological Resolution Examples

Use these examples with `constitution.md` section 6.4 and the validation sections in `chapter-template.md` and `human-review-rubric.md`.

Christological resolution is not a devotional flourish. It is the final movement of an argument in which Christ exposes the false order, bears or unmasks the false sacrifice, disarms the powers, restores the created good, and reforms persons and communities by the Spirit.

## Strong Resolution Pattern

A strong resolution usually does five things:

1. Names how Christ exposes the false order.
2. Shows how the cross bears, unmasks, or ends the false sacrifice.
3. Shows how Christ disarms the powers rather than merely replacing one power with another.
4. Restores the created good under its true vocation.
5. Describes the re-formation of human practice, desire, institution, or community.

## Worked Example: Corrupted Law

Improper movement:

> Legalism is bad, but Jesus gives grace, so we should trust Jesus.

Why it fails:

- It names Jesus but does not show what Christ exposes.
- It does not identify the false sacrifice demanded by legalism.
- It leaves law itself either despised or unexplained.

Stronger movement:

> Christ exposes legalism as the corruption of law into self-justification. At the cross, he bears the curse that legalism tries to manage and unmasks the false sacrifice of anxious performance. In his resurrection and gift of the Spirit, law is restored as witness, wisdom, and love's form rather than a ladder of self-salvation. The person and community are re-formed from defensive proving into grateful obedience.

Why it works:

- The created good, law, is restored rather than rejected.
- The false sacrifice is named.
- Christ's cross, resurrection, and Spirit all do real argumentative work.

## Worked Example: Corrupted Institution

Improper movement:

> Institutions fail, but Jesus loves us and we should be faithful.

Why it fails:

- It does not distinguish institution as created good from institutional corruption.
- It does not show how Christ judges the self-protective institution.
- It gives a true sentiment without resolving the argument.

Stronger movement:

> Christ exposes the institution that has made preservation its lord. At the cross, he receives the violence of self-protective power without answering it in kind, revealing the lie that truth must be sacrificed for order. His resurrection disarms the institution's threat of exclusion and loss. In the Spirit, institutional life can be restored as truthful service, accountable authority, repentant memory, and care for the vulnerable.

Why it works:

- The institution is not treated as evil by origin.
- The sacrificial demand is identified as truth and the vulnerable.
- Restoration includes concrete institutional practices.

## Worked Example: Political Worship

Improper movement:

> Politics becomes idolatrous, but Christ is King.

Why it fails:

- The statement is doctrinally true but underdeveloped.
- It does not expose the specific false political order.
- It does not name what political worship sacrifices.

Stronger movement:

> Christ exposes political worship by refusing the nation's claim to final loyalty. At the cross, he unmasks the sacrificial logic by which public bodies preserve themselves through enemies, scapegoats, and sacred violence. His resurrection disarms the terror of imperial judgment and restores political order to limited service under God. The community formed by his Spirit learns truthful speech, enemy love, public courage, and refusal to offer bodies to a false savior.

Why it works:

- It names political worship's sacrificial system.
- It shows how Christ disarms rather than merely competes.
- It describes communal re-formation.

## Common Improper Resolutions

### Decorative Jesus Sentence

Pattern:

> This system is corrupt. We need Jesus.

Problem: true as far as it goes, but it does not show how Christ resolves the argument.

### Therapeutic Closure

Pattern:

> This system wounds people. Healing is possible when we become whole.

Problem: healing language may be useful, but canonical resolution must be explicitly Christological rather than merely therapeutic.

### Moral Exhortation

Pattern:

> This system is unjust. Therefore we must do better.

Problem: moral exhortation cannot replace Christ's exposure, judgment, disarming, restoration, and re-formation.

### Political Replacement

Pattern:

> This power is corrupt. A better movement must take control.

Problem: replacing one power with another does not show how Christ disarms the fallen logic of power.

### Doom Without Restoration

Pattern:

> The corruption is total, the system is doomed, and judgment is coming.

Problem: prophetic warning without restoration risks despair, superiority, or spectacle.

## Reviewer Checklist

- [ ] Does the resolution identify what Christ exposes?
- [ ] Does it name the false sacrifice and show how the cross unmasks or bears it?
- [ ] Does it show how Christ disarms the powers?
- [ ] Does it restore the created good rather than merely condemn corruption?
- [ ] Does it describe re-formed desire, practice, community, or institution?
- [ ] Does it avoid a decorative final sentence about Jesus?
- [ ] Does it avoid merely therapeutic, moralistic, political, or doom-shaped closure?
