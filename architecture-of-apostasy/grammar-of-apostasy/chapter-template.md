## 5. Created Good

- What is good by creation, vocation, or divine gift?
- What is its proper service to God, neighbor, and creation?
- What must not be denied or despised when critiquing its corruption?

## 6. Governing Power

Describe the power, office, institution, symbolic order, or principality entrusted with the created good.

- What domain does it govern?
- What legitimate authority or formative role does it have?
- What evidence supports this identification?

## 7. Fall Of The Power

Trace how the governing power becomes self-referential.

- How does it seek preservation, worship, immunity, or control?
- What symbols, offices, habits, laws, or rituals are captured?
- What rival eschatology or promise of salvation appears?

## 8. Disordered Logos

Name the false symbolic narrative proclaimed by the fallen power.

- What false diagnosis does it offer?
- What false promise does it make?
- What false anthropology or soteriology does it teach?
- What desires does it train?

## 18. Validation Checklist

- [ ] The created good is named before its corruption.
- [ ] Canonical terms are used consistently.
- [ ] Claim types are distinguished.
- [ ] Scripture anchors are identified and handled with exegetical restraint.
- [ ] Historical claims are sourced proportionately.
- [ ] Contemporary claims are dated and verified.
- [ ] Medical, psychological, trauma, addiction, or abuse claims include appropriate cautions.
- [ ] At least one serious objection or rival reading is included.
- [ ] The chapter does not confuse prophetic warning with omniscient verdict.
- [ ] The chapter does not turn tools, documents, AI, symbols, or institutions into sources of ultimate authority.
- [ ] The Christological restoration section shows exposure, sacrifice, disarming, restoration, and reformation.
- [ ] Diagram notes are included when visual modeling is needed.
- [ ] Cross-references are accurate.
- [ ] Bibliography or source notes are sufficient for the claim load.
- [ ] Human editorial review is recorded.
