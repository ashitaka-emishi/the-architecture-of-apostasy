# Theological Validation Checklist

Use this checklist before a chapter, diagram, prompt, schema, or concept becomes canonical.

## 1. Ontology

- [ ] The created good is identified before its corruption is described.
- [ ] Canonical terms are used consistently.
- [ ] Logos and Disordered Logos are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] Principalities are not reduced to merely human institutions unless the chapter explicitly limits itself to institutional analysis.
- [ ] The Chemical Temple is not treated as medical determinism.
- [ ] Sacrifice is traced to its object of worship.
- [ ] Restoration is Christological rather than merely therapeutic, political, or moralistic.

## 2. Method

- [ ] The chapter begins with Scripture or explains why it does not.
- [ ] Biblical teaching, historical theology, and speculative synthesis are distinguished.
- [ ] High-risk claims are classified.
- [ ] Objections or rival readings are included.
- [ ] The chapter avoids sensationalism.
- [ ] The chapter avoids binding the reader's conscience where the project is speculating.

## 3. Research

- [ ] Biblical citations include book, chapter, and verse.
- [ ] Historical claims cite primary sources when reasonably available.
- [ ] Contemporary claims are dated and verified.
- [ ] Quotes are accurate and not decontextualized.
- [ ] AI-generated claims have been checked by a human editor.
- [ ] Bibliography or source notes are sufficient for the claims made.

## 4. Christological Resolution

- [ ] Christ exposes the false order.
- [ ] Christ bears, unmasks, or ends the false sacrifice.
- [ ] Christ disarms the powers.
- [ ] Christ restores the created good.
- [ ] The resolution is not a decorative ending detached from the argument.

## 5. Pastoral Risk

- [ ] The work does not shame the afflicted.
- [ ] Medical, psychological, legal, or financial claims are appropriately qualified.
- [ ] The work avoids despair and cynicism.
- [ ] The rhetoric serves repentance and restoration rather than contempt.
- [ ] Scapegoat analysis does not create a new scapegoat.

## 6. Diagram Validation

- [ ] Diagram nodes use canonical labels.
- [ ] Edges have clear directionality.
- [ ] Color is not the only carrier of meaning.
- [ ] Caption explains what the diagram can and cannot prove.
- [ ] Diagram links back to a section of the chapter.

## 7. Agent Output Validation

- [ ] Agent role is named.
- [ ] Inputs are listed.
- [ ] Claims are classified.
- [ ] Citations are provided or missing citations are flagged.
- [ ] Risks and open questions are included.
- [ ] Recommended next action is concrete.

## 8. Integration Decision

- [ ] Accept.
- [ ] Accept with revisions.
- [ ] Hold for more research.
- [ ] Reject.
