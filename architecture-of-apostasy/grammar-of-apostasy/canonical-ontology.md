# The Grammar of Apostasy: Canonical Ontology

These are the controlling terms for the project. Essays may develop or qualify them, but should not redefine them casually.

Use this file with `constitution.md`, especially section 16 on versioning rules and section 19 on the canonical glossary.

## Logos

Definition: God's ordering Word through whom creation exists, coheres, is judged, and is restored. In explicitly Christian usage, the Logos is finally identified with Jesus Christ.

Examples:

- The divine Word by whom creation is ordered.
- Christ as the true center of meaning, judgment, and restoration.
- Scripture's witness to the Word through whom all things were made.

Non-examples:

- A generic principle of rationality detached from Christ.
- A private ideology that gives a community coherence.
- A human institution's mission statement, even when the language is religious.

Usage caution: use `Logos` for God's true ordering Word. Use `false logos`, `disordered logos`, or `counterfeit logos` for rival symbolic orders.

## Creation

Definition: the good order made by God, including material, spiritual, personal, communal, institutional, ecological, symbolic, and historical realities.

Examples:

- Bodies, families, memory, nations, law, work, desire, sacred time, language, and institutions as created goods.
- Material reality received as gift rather than rejected as evil.
- Social structures considered according to vocation before corruption.

Non-examples:

- A neutral raw material with no divine vocation.
- A fallen system treated as evil by origin.
- A private fantasy world used to evade creaturely limits.

Usage caution: name creation's goodness before diagnosing its corruption.

## Created Good

Definition: any reality, capacity, structure, symbol, institution, or vocation that is good by origin and divine intention before it is distorted by sin.

Examples:

- Law ordered toward justice.
- Temple ordered toward worship.
- Nation ordered toward public order and neighbor care.
- Desire ordered toward God and rightly loved goods.

Non-examples:

- Mammon as an idol.
- A propaganda system that exists to preserve falsehood.
- An addiction pattern treated as if it were the created good itself.

Usage caution: do not confuse a corrupted form with the created good it has captured.

## Principality

Definition: a created governing power intended to order a domain of creation toward God. The term may name spiritual, institutional, symbolic, or mixed forms of governance depending on evidence and genre.

Examples:

- Political authority ordered toward justice.
- Ecclesial authority ordered toward worship, discipline, and care.
- Economic authority ordered toward provision and stewardship.
- Symbolic authority that shapes public meaning.

Non-examples:

- Every large organization automatically labeled a principality.
- A merely personal temptation with no governing domain.
- A demonological explanation used to avoid historical evidence.

Usage caution: do not collapse principalities into merely human institutions, and do not invoke them without analysis.

## Fall Of The Powers

Definition: the movement by which created governing powers become self-referential, demand ultimate loyalty, and reorganize their domains around preservation rather than service to God.

Examples:

- A church structure protecting reputation instead of truth and repentance.
- A state demanding sacred loyalty and sacrificial violence for its own permanence.
- A market system treating human bodies as fuel for endless growth.

Non-examples:

- Any exercise of authority.
- Institutional weakness or error without self-protective worship demand.
- Legitimate discipline ordered toward restoration.

Usage caution: diagnose the fall by markers such as self-reference, worship demand, sacrificial demand, immunity from critique, and resistance to Christological exposure.

## Disordered Logos

Definition: the false symbolic order proclaimed by fallen powers. It explains the world, assigns guilt, offers salvation, identifies enemies, demands sacrifice, and trains desire.

Examples:

- "Security requires silence" inside an abusive institution.
- "Consumption will save you" inside a consumer order.
- "The nation redeems suffering through victory" inside political worship.

Non-examples:

- Any incorrect idea.
- A single mistaken statement without formative power.
- A medical diagnosis or biological disease.

Usage caution: capitalize `Disordered Logos` when referring to the project concept. Mark speculative uses clearly.

## Fallen Liturgy

Definition: an embodied pattern of ritual, habit, speech, policy, technology, economics, or institution that incarnates a Disordered Logos and forms people into its worship.

Examples:

- Public rituals that sacralize political enemies.
- Institutional reporting structures that train silence.
- Consumer habits that make desire endlessly restless.
- Digital practices that train attention toward outrage and comparison.

Non-examples:

- Any repeated action.
- A neutral habit with no formative allegiance.
- Historic Christian liturgy merely because it is embodied and repeated.

Usage caution: identify what the practice forms, what it protects, and what it sacrifices.

## Chemical Temple

Definition: the embodied human person formed through memory, attention, desire, neurochemistry, habit, emotion, trauma, imagination, and worship.

Examples:

- Addiction shaping craving, attention, and bodily expectation.
- Fear-based institutions training nervous systems toward preservation.
- Repeated prayer, fasting, and mercy reshaping desire toward God.

Non-examples:

- A claim that chemistry alone explains the person.
- Medical advice.
- A deterministic account that denies agency, grace, or pastoral care.

Usage caution: this framework may draw from psychology and embodied formation, but must not present itself as medical advice.

## Sacrifice

Definition: the impulse toward self-offering, true when ordered to God and distorted when given to idols, false powers, or systems of preservation.

Examples:

- Christian self-offering in love of God and neighbor.
- Scapegoating a vulnerable person to preserve institutional peace.
- Sacrificing truth, bodies, children, enemies, or conscience to preserve a false order.

Non-examples:

- Any cost or inconvenience.
- Cruelty baptized as holiness.
- Self-harm treated as spiritual maturity.

Usage caution: distinguish cruciform sacrifice from sacrifice demanded by a false lord.

## Scapegoat

Definition: the person or group onto whom a fallen system transfers guilt so it can preserve itself without repentance.

Examples:

- A whistleblower blamed for exposing corruption.
- A minority group made to carry a nation's anxiety.
- A family member designated as the problem to protect the family myth.

Non-examples:

- Any person who receives consequences for actual wrongdoing.
- Accountability rightly ordered toward truth and restoration.
- A rhetorical opponent one dislikes.

Usage caution: do not use scapegoat language to erase real agency or flatten complex responsibility.

## Nehushtan

Definition: a holy instrument detached from God and treated as a controllable object of power.

Examples:

- A document treated as authority apart from the truth it serves.
- A ritual preserved as identity while repentance is refused.
- An AI tool trusted as theological authority rather than servant.

Non-examples:

- A symbol used humbly as a sign.
- A document that remains accountable to Scripture, truth, and human review.
- A tradition received with gratitude and repentance.

Usage caution: the right response is not contempt for instruments, but returning instruments to service under God.

## Christological Resolution

Definition: the final movement of an argument in which Christ exposes, judges, disarms, and restores the corrupted order under analysis.

Examples:

- Showing how the cross unmasks a false sacrifice.
- Showing how resurrection restores the created good rather than merely condemning corruption.
- Showing how the Spirit reforms persons and communities into truthful embodiment.

Non-examples:

- A decorative final sentence about Jesus.
- A moral lesson without the cross, resurrection, Spirit, or restoration.
- A conclusion that diagnoses collapse but never turns toward redemption.

Usage caution: every canonical chapter must resolve in Christ, not merely mention Christ.

## Restoration

Definition: Christ's work of exposing false orders, disarming powers, healing persons, and returning created goods to their proper end.

Examples:

- Repentance that changes embodied practice.
- Institutions reformed toward truth, mercy, justice, and accountability.
- Desire retrained toward God and neighbor through the Spirit.

Non-examples:

- Nostalgia for an earlier fallen arrangement.
- Cosmetic institutional repair that preserves the false lord.
- Vague optimism without judgment, repentance, or re-embodiment.

Usage caution: restoration includes judgment and mercy; do not reduce it to either accusation or sentiment.

## Migration Note Convention

Every change to a canonical term must include a migration note alongside the change. Use this format in pull requests, changelogs, or the changed grammar document:

```text
Migration note:
- Term:
- Previous definition:
- Proposed definition:
- Reason for change:
- Affected chapters or artifacts:
- Required updates:
- Backward compatibility:
```

Use a major version change for ontology, methodology, or project-identity changes; a minor version change for new terms or sections; and a patch change for clarifications that do not alter meaning.
