# Human Review Rubric

Use this rubric before accepting a chapter, diagram, prompt, schema, roadmap item, or other artifact as canonical Grammar of Apostasy work.

The automated checks can find missing files, stale paths, and some consistency failures. Human review must judge theological coherence, source discipline, pastoral risk, and whether the work truly resolves in Christ.

## Review Scale

For each category, mark one decision:

- Pass: ready for canonical use.
- Revise: promising but needs targeted revision.
- Hold: not ready; requires more research, review, or reframing.
- Reject: unsuitable for canonical integration.

## 1. Canonical Terms

Pass criteria:

- Terms match `canonical-ontology.md` and `constitution.md`.
- New terms are clearly proposed rather than silently introduced.
- The work does not redefine Logos, Disordered Logos, principality, Chemical Temple, sacrifice, or restoration casually.

Revision triggers:

- Terms are used loosely.
- A term changes meaning across sections.
- A new term lacks a migration note or definition.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

## 2. Created Good

Pass criteria:

- The created good is identified before corruption is described.
- The work preserves the goodness of creation.
- The critique does not become gnostic, anti-body, anti-institutional, or anti-creational.

Revision triggers:

- The corrupted form is treated as evil by origin.
- The created good is vague or absent.
- Restoration is impossible because the good has not been named.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

Notes:

## 3. Corruption And Disordered Logos

Pass criteria:

- The work names the relevant false symbolic order or Disordered Logos.
- The fall of the governing power is traced with evidence and restraint.
- The account distinguishes error, weakness, corruption, and apostasy.

Revision triggers:

- Every problem is treated as total apostasy.
- The Disordered Logos is asserted but not described.
- The work confuses intensity with proof.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

## 4. Fallen Liturgies And Embodied Formation

Pass criteria:

- The work identifies repeated practices, habits, rituals, policies, or technologies that form persons and communities.
- The Chemical Temple framework is used without medical determinism.
- Embodied formation is connected to worship, desire, and practice.

Revision triggers:

- Formation claims are abstract and not embodied.
- Medical, psychological, trauma, addiction, or abuse claims are unqualified.
- The work shames afflicted people rather than naming false formation.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

Notes:

## 5. Sacrificial System

Pass criteria:

- The work identifies what the false order demands in sacrifice.
- Sacrifice is connected to the object of worship.
- Scapegoat analysis does not create a new scapegoat.

Revision triggers:

- Sacrifice is treated as any cost rather than worship-shaped offering.
- Victims, scapegoats, or demanded silences are not named.
- The work baptizes cruelty as faithfulness.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

Notes:

## 6. Claim Discipline And Sources

Pass criteria:

- Doctrine, exegesis, historical judgment, interpretation, synthesis, speculation, and pastoral counsel are distinguishable.
- Sources are proportionate to the claim load.
- Contemporary claims are dated and verified.
- Serious rival readings or objections are included.

Revision triggers:

- Historical claims lack visible support.
- Speculation is unmarked.
- Rival readings are dismissed rather than answered.
- Quotes or examples are decontextualized.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

## 7. Christological Resolution

Pass criteria:

- Christ exposes the false order.
- Christ bears, unmasks, or ends the false sacrifice.
- Christ disarms the powers.
- Christ restores the created good.
- Christ reforms persons and communities through the Spirit.

Revision triggers:

- Jesus is mentioned only decoratively.
- The ending is therapeutic, political, or moralistic without Christological restoration.
- The work diagnoses collapse but does not turn toward redemption.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject

Notes:

## 8. Pastoral And Rhetorical Risk

Pass criteria:

- The work serves repentance and restoration rather than contempt.
- It avoids despair, cynicism, theatrical accusation, and superiority.
- It includes cautions where readers could be harmed by overgeneralization.

Revision triggers:

- The rhetoric humiliates the afflicted.
- Institution-wounded readers are pushed toward permanent suspicion.
- The work confuses prophetic warning with omniscient verdict.

Reviewer decision:

- [ ] Pass
- [ ] Revise
- [ ] Hold
- [ ] Reject
