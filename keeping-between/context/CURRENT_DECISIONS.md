# Current Decisions

This file records current decisions that should override older assistant formulations or source assumptions during new work. Update only after explicit human approval.

## D-001 — Apostolic greeting

Use this exact structure:

**Speaker 1:** *Before throne and pasture.*  
**Speaker 2:** *Before fang and fleece.*  
**Both:** ***The Troth remains!***

The split matters: neither speaker possesses the whole utterance alone; the common confession is spoken together.

In a communal enactment, either speaking part may be voiced by a group and the final confession may widen to the whole assembly. D-036 uses the distinct Greeting of Departure within *Aru Va'en*; it does not alter this Apostolic Greeting elsewhere.

## D-002 — No Order of the Troth

Do not introduce a canonical institution called **the Order of the Troth**. The earlier assistant use of that name was corrected and is removed from active project vocabulary except when discussing frozen source provenance.

There is no order yet. The present work concerns the birthing of a thing: a vocation, discipline, and possible gathered form called **The Keeping Between**. If an order ever arises, its name will be **The Keeping Between**, and even then it must remain provisional, accountable, and capable of passing away when its charge is complete.

## D-003 — Unclaimed Virtue

The doctrine does **not** mean virtue is unreal or that Christ lacks morally pure positions. Christ is the measure. The doctrine concerns human self-certification and the danger of identifying one's own moral self-conception with the Good.

## D-004 — Authority

Authority is **entrusted, not possessed**. Legitimate authority can arise from office, competence, experience, witness, calling, relationship, or necessity. Authority may determine who must decide without determining who is right. Greater authority means greater accountability.

## D-005 — Repentance

The core exchange is:

**Returning:** *I did not remain.*  
**Keeper:** *You are not the measure.*  
**Both:** ***The Troth remains.***

Repentance does not erase harm. Return includes confession, truth, restitution where possible, accountability, and changed practice.

## D-006 — Redemption of the fallen

The myth must leave genuine redemption open to the fallen. The fallen are not identical with their fall. Redemption cannot be cheap absolution and cannot require denial of what happened.

## D-007 — Violence revision direction

Do not pretend violence does not exist. However, the active revision direction is to remove violence as the myth's resolving mechanism and make the core struggle **peaceful resistance to physical and spiritual violence**, while preserving the intensity of the assault.

## D-008 — Black Sun

Preserve the Black Sun's force as exposure, accusation, psychological/spiritual assault, and deformation. Morning must not simply erase what the Black Sun revealed; morning transforms by truthful keeping and return.

## D-009 — Aruvan / keeping

The Aruvan and every keeper remain dependent. Preserve the principle:

> **Even the keeper must be kept. Nothing faithful keeps alone.**

The Shepherd keeps what keeps. No office or ancestral body is self-sufficient.

## D-010 — Book of the Troth

Preferred working architecture: a **New-Testament-shaped collection of letters from Vaerun to companions**, with possible circular letters and remembered sayings. Theology should arise from concrete pastoral situations rather than an omniscient treatise.

## D-011 — Vaerun's authority

Vaerun's authority should arise especially from **witness, responsibility, experience, and service**, not claimed holiness. He must remain correctable and capable of confession.

## D-012 — Troth/Christ relation

Do not make the Troth a separate metaphysical principle parallel to Christ. A strong working formulation is that the Troth names enduring fidelity to the Good revealed in Christ. Continue to test the exact formulation against Scripture and the internal mythic line that the Shepherd is prior to the Troth.

## D-013 — Guardian/Troth append module status

The Guardian/Troth append package incorporated on 2026-08-08 is development material and working synthesis, not frozen source text and not a promotion of every claim to `user_established`. Its workstream documents belong in `doctrine/`, `aru-vaen/`, and `book-of-wolves/`, never in `source/`.

## D-014 — Guardian offices as working synthesis

Vaelun, Vaerun, and Hrovan are distinct offices of keeping rather than a simple promotion ladder. Vaelun is primarily guardian of threshold or boundary; Vaerun is guardian of the veil who may need to cross the boundary in order to remain faithful; Hrovan bears the hall or center and is closest to the throne-guardian archetype. All guardian offices remain under the Troth and subject to the Shepherd.

This direction is established and refined by D-024.

## D-015 — Aruvan as accumulated keeping

Aruvan is not a fourth guardian rank. As working synthesis, Aruvan names accumulated keeping across generations: memory, tradition, oath, sacrifice, responsibility, institutional identity, and corporate guardianship taking form. Its core temptation is to infer present righteousness from accumulated past fidelity; its redemption depends on discovering that relinquishment is not abandonment because even the keeper must be kept. D-027 establishes that `Aruvann` was only a misspelling, not a distinct commentary term or being.

## D-016 — Fang and fleece symmetry

Fang does not confer guilt; fleece does not confer virtue. Guardian strength can fall into domination, possession, sacralized duty, and self-justification. Vulnerability and suffering can fall into dependence, false innocence, moral sovereignty, outsourced violence, and sanctified victimhood. Both keeper and kept remain under the Troth and require the Shepherd.

## D-017 — The Troth and historical forms

As working synthesis, the Troth is prior fidelity or right relation and is not reducible to loyalty, obedience, institutional continuity, tradition, sincerity, or mere perseverance. “The Troth remains” does not mean every historical form must remain. Apostasy can occur through continued fidelity to a form whose relation to the Good has been lost, and the Troth makes repentance possible because fidelity is deeper than the historical forms that once embodied it.

## D-018 — The Shepherd

The Shepherd is explicitly Christological and is not the strongest guardian, highest throne, or infinite Aruvan. The Shepherd names a categorically different sovereignty: keeping without possession, judgment without annihilation, authority without cultivated dependence, and restoration without denial of injury. The Shepherd judges both fang and fleece and makes created relinquishment possible because created guardians are not the ultimate source of keeping.

## D-019 — Letters of the Troth append framework

Letters must remain epistolary situations rather than miniature doctrinal essays. Preferred working progression: guardianship, fang/fleece, Aruvan, Troth, Shepherd, return, Vaerun relinquishment, then communal or multiple final voices. Vaerun should learn, revise, and sometimes confess error through the correspondence; he should not receive the final word.

## D-020 — Aru Va'en source priority

The frozen PDF, `source/aru-vaen/Aru vaen-A Keeping of the Troth.pdf`, is the definitive myth source over the recovered V3 draft. V3 may be consulted as a record of alternate development, but it does not control wording, structure, continuity, or theology where it diverges from the PDF. Any future use of a V3 variant must be recorded as an explicit revision of the PDF baseline, never introduced through silent merging.

## D-021 — Little Vey survives and writes to Vaerun

Little Vey survives the Keeping and remains a living, embodied companion in the post-Keeping chronology. He later writes to Vaerun. The revised myth must not kill him, reduce him to a spectral or ancestral presence, or leave his survival ambiguous. D-052 settles his single lasting wound; his actions during the ordeal are governed by D-040 through D-044. The recovered source letter `source/book-of-the-troth/The Letter of Little Vey to the Vaerun.md` now supplies the content of his warning concerning stature, fame, correction, and the danger of Vaerun becoming the measure; Vaerun's response and the letter's final placement remain open.

## D-022 — The Keeping Between is provisional if it becomes an order at all

No order has been formed or established. The active institutional materials must no longer propose an **Order of the Troth**. They may explore **The Keeping Between** as a provisional order only if that is what the vocation's birthing requires.

The Keeping Between is not the name of a permanent religious body. It names a vocation produced by a particular historical condition: an age in which the old structures are passing away and the new ones must not be enthroned prematurely. Its charge is to hear what must not be forgotten, guard what must not be surrendered, discern what is being born, and remain until the passage is complete.

The existence of drafts does not create an institution, membership, office, or authority. D-002 remains controlling. Provisional order-language may appear only inside clearly labeled conditional drafts and must not imply that The Keeping Between already exists as a settled body.

## D-023 — The myth and the Shepherd remain central

*Aru Va'en* remains the project's central symbolic narrative, with the Shepherd at its Christological center. Institutional drafts are downstream applications of the myth and may not redefine its offices, symbols, or theology. The Shepherd, not a fellowship, office, Rule, Constitution, or Sigil, remains the center and measure of keeping.

## D-024 — Guardian offices are distinct callings

Vaelun, Vaerun, and Hrovan name different kinds of calling, not ranks or stages in a ladder. Vaelun keeps the threshold; Vaerun is set at the veil and bound to cross when fidelity requires it; Hrovan bears the hall or center, including responsibility near concentrated authority. `Hrovann` in the recovered institutional documents is a misspelling of `Hrovan`. General sustained responsibility belongs to keeping as such and does not redefine Vaerun.

The active myth revision must remove foreshadowing that treats Vaerun as a future Hrovan. A person may receive different callings during a life, but such a change is neither promotion nor the narrative destiny of Vaerun.

## D-025 — The myth's form, vocation, and final review

The revised *Aru Va'en* must retain the old, liturgical feel of the definitive myth. Its oral cadence, ritual repetition, elemental imagery, solemnity, and remembered formulas are part of its work rather than ornament to be modernized away.

The myth should itself be a keeper of the Troth: it preserves entrusted memory, resists false possession, and calls its hearers back beneath the Shepherd. This is a subordinate mythic and liturgical vocation. It does not make the myth Scripture, revelation, the measure of the Good, or a possessor of the Troth. The myth must itself remain kept and judged by the Shepherd.

No material revision or complete rewrite receives final acceptance until a thorough Scriptural review has tested its major claims and symbols and maintained a strong Christological center. The review must distinguish exegesis from constructive theology and mythopoesis, preserve rival readings and inferential steps, document unresolved tensions, and return the result to the user as final theological and literary authority.

## D-026 — The Shepherd has no staff

The active *Aru Va'en* revision must not give the Shepherd a staff. The staff appears only in the non-controlling V3 draft and is absent from the definitive PDF. It creates an unwanted fantasy-wizard or armed-guardian silhouette and must not be imported into the active myth. Show the Shepherd's keeping through his embodied presence, scarred hands, voice, movement, judgment, protection, gathering, and the room he makes.

## D-027 — Aruvan spelling and opening prose

`Aruvann` is a misspelling of `Aruvan`, not a second term, being, office, or commentary category. Use `Aruvan` throughout active project material. Frozen source artifacts remain unchanged.

In the active myth, reduce the repeated use of `old`. Keep it where age, inheritance, ritual memory, or long duration carries real weight; do not use it merely to manufacture atmosphere or replace it mechanically with another repeated synonym.

Reduce the cluster of `fear` statements in the opening. Vaerun hesitates because he knows that answering the cry and crossing the veil will require costly fidelity, and he wrongly believes he must be sufficient to measure what faithfulness requires. He fears that he will fail to be that measure. The later revelation corrects the burden itself: Vaerun is not the measure; the Shepherd is. Fear may remain embodied in his reluctance, breath, trembling, and silence, but the prose should not repeatedly explain it before the scene has carried it.

## D-028 — Little Vey's summons to remembrance

The active *Letter of Little Vey to the Vaerun* should center Vey's beckoning as Vaerun becomes great in the sight of others:

> Remember where we stood.
>
> Remember when only we knew your name.
>
> Remember where the Shepherd stood.

The letter is about continuing to remember the Troth as one's greatness among others increases. This remembrance is not nostalgia, a claim to private access, or another credential of greatness. It returns Vaerun to shared dependence, to the cry, and to the Shepherd as the center and measure. D-029 establishes that the letter addresses the change from **a Vaerun** to **the Vaerun**. The active revision belongs at `book-of-wolves/letters/LITTLE_VEY_TO_VAERUN.md`; the recovered source letter remains frozen and unchanged.

## D-029 — A Vaerun and the Vaerun

Before the hill and the encounter with the Shepherd, the narrator is **a Vaerun**: one bearer of the crossing vocation among others who have borne or may bear it. After the hill, his keeping becomes widely remembered and the many come to call him **the Vaerun**.

This is not a new office, a promotion, an ontological elevation, or proof of superior righteousness. It is singular public stature formed around his witness. Little Vey's letter addresses precisely this danger: the many may turn a shared calling into a title only one person can answer, identify Vaerun's judgment with faithfulness, and forget where the companions and the Shepherd stood. Vey calls him to receive the vocation while refusing possession of the singular greatness attached to it.

## D-030 — Least-harm resistance and the Shepherd's grief

The wolves' peaceful resistance follows this least-harm refrain:

> Do not hit when holding is enough.
>
> Do not maim when hitting is enough.

Holding, shielding, interposition, carrying, and opening escape come first. When holding is no longer enough to stop an immediate assault, the wolves may use body, shoulder, or controlled impact to throw attackers back and restore protective space. They must not use fang or claw to draw an attacker's blood, and they must not maim, kill, punish, pursue, or take vengeance. The resisting wolves may themselves bleed; their restraint does not make the danger painless or unreal.

When a wolf falters in restraint, the Shepherd's grief becomes visibly greater. His grief exposes the failure without becoming magical punishment, emotional coercion, or proof that the grieving observers are righteous. The Shepherd remains the wounded center who sees both the threatened and the attacker as persons and keeps open the possibility of protection, truth, accountability, and return.

## D-031 — The Living Lamb remains related to Aruvan

Retain **the Living Lamb** and its essential relation to **Aruvan**. Aruvan is the form made by generations of keeping: accumulated memory, responsibility, sacrifice, and guardianship. The Living Lamb is the corresponding gathered life of those who have been kept, wounded, scattered, and brought into active companionship without being possessed or merely carried.

The earlier active word `consort` is deprecated by D-056 because it implies two separately personified partners and carries unwanted royal or nuptial associations. The durable decision is that Aruvan and the Living Lamb belong to one creaturely communion beneath the Shepherd. Neither is Christ, the measure, a fourth guardian office, or an independent saving power.

The individual lamb on the hill remains an embodied creature rather than a disguised Christ. D-056 controls how that creature participates in the larger Living Lamb and how the twofold relation with Aruvan becomes visible.

## D-032 — The Shepherd is recognizable, not announced or hidden

Do not place a John 10:11 superscription or other explanatory identification before the myth. The Shepherd's identity is not a secret, puzzle, or final-act twist, but it also need not be announced before the story can show him.

Make the Shepherd recognizably Christological from his first appearance through his wounded human body, scarred hands, relation to the lamb, self-giving protection, grief, judgment, calling by name, reception of return, and gathering without possession.

> **Vey's ending names his priority rather than unmasking him.**

The closing words name the Shepherd's priority over every creaturely account of the Troth and bring the myth's theological order into speech.

## D-033 — The Shepherd's continual call begins return

During the struggle, the Shepherd continually calls his flock. The call is not reserved for the end of the conflict and does not wait upon the wolves' success. It reaches toward the lost within the struggle and begins their return when they hear, recognize, and turn toward him.

The call does not compel a response, certify completed repentance, erase injury, or remove the need for boundaries and repair. One lost sheep hears the Shepherd, begins to remember, and answers with a cry too soft for the other wolves to hear. Little Vey hears the cry and smells remembrance, but he does not name the sheep or explain what he has noticed. He seeks Vaerun. Vey's seeking alerts Vaerun first, and Vaerun's changed attention then alerts the others. The Shepherd remains the one who calls the lost by name, and the circle may open to receive and protect the one who turns, but these acts answer the Shepherd's prior call rather than originate return.

## D-034 — The wolves' circle mirrors the Shepherd's work

During the struggle, the Shepherd works within the circle while the wolves hold its boundary. The wolves' work at the circumference must mirror the Shepherd's prior and continuing work within. His continual calling and their least-harm resistance are settled parts of that correspondence. D-065 settles the central spatial grammar: the Shepherd makes room within bodies by separating without severing and reordering members caught in the four curses, while the wolves make room between bodies through least-harm restraint and opened paths. D-066 settles the shared public recognition through the first returning sheep's opened eye.

The mirroring is not equality. The Shepherd is the source and measure of the pattern; the wolves imitate it partially, bodily, and fallibly. Their resistance neither completes nor enables his keeping. When they turn holding into punishment or protection into vengeance, they cease to mirror him, and his visible grief reveals the break.

## D-035 — Response to the candidate who declines

In the Calling of the Truvane, place the unnamed candidate after Murn rather than beside the eager wolf who is passed over. The candidate's shaking legs and breaking voice show that he is frightened and not ready for this crossing. The Gravane gives him the same brow-to-brow gesture used in the calling, and he says exactly:

> **Not this crossing.**

The Gravane does not withdraw the touch and responds exactly:

> **None of us is the measure.**

The response is a shared creaturely confession rather than condemnation. The Gravane does not speak the candidate's name for the crossing and then moves on. The candidate remains among the Truvane without shame, replacement ritual, or required explanation. He continues to answer the subsequent callings and, in the departure greeting, speaks **"Though the road divide us"** with the Hrovan and those who remain before joining the whole hall in **"The Troth remains!"**

The response does not revoke the decline or compel the candidate to cross. It denies that either assent or refusal, personal strength or self-judged insufficiency, is the measure. The Shepherd remains the measure. The candidate's decline is not depicted as failure, sin, apostasy, moral superiority, or a completed repentance rite. Crossing is one calling, not the test of fidelity.

## D-036 — Departure greeting in the hall

Immediately before those called to cross leave the hall, the Greeting of Departure is spoken collectively:

**Hrovan and the gathered Truvane:** *Though the road divide us.*

**Those called to cross:** *Though the night conceal us.*

**The whole hall:** ***The Troth remains!***

Those called to cross are Vaerun and the twelve named Vaelun. The whole hall includes those who remain and those who leave. The widening voices preserve the settled three-part structure while making division and uncertainty, rather than role-symbols, the particular burden of this departure. Neither the hall, the guardians, nor any one office possesses the complete confession. The Apostolic Greeting under D-001 remains unchanged for its other uses.

## D-037 — Hrovan's uncertainty and the communal objection

Before the commission and calling, the Hrovan publicly distinguishes the obligation to answer the cry from certainty about what faithful action requires. He says exactly:

> **The cry binds us to answer. It does not tell us what answer will keep faith.**

The admission does not withdraw the Hrovan from responsibility. He remains responsible for convening discernment and commissioning action, but his office does not make his first interpretation righteous.

One of the gathered Gravane then voices the communal objection:

> **The cry has told us whom we may not abandon. It has not told us whom to strike.**

The objection changes the commission toward witness, protection, and discernment rather than merely being heard and dismissed. It does not make the Gravane infallible; it prevents the community from treating the cry itself as permission for violence.

## D-038 — Assent through character, not twelve speeches

The twelve companions do not each receive a separate verbal response. Their differentiated gestures, postures, fears, habits, and brief source-established exchanges carry their assent. Preserve the communal **"Aru va'en"** as the ritual response to each naming without adding a uniform spoken answer from every companion.

Do not lose the source's calling portraits of Harrow, Murn, or Little Vey:

- Harrow's torn ear, trembling, and brow-to-brow calling remain; he does not need to speak.
- Murn's age, rough breath, grin, and brief exchange with the Gravane remain; his humor must not be flattened into solemn compliance.
- Little Vey's trembling, his look toward Vaerun, his whispered **"Vaerun,"** and the Gravane's recognition of why he is called remain.

The other companions may assent through concise embodied details already belonging to their characters. Limiting speech preserves the old liturgical cadence and prevents the calling from becoming twelve declarations of individual worthiness.

## D-039 — Physical grammar of the circle

The circle is permeable rather than sealed. It opens inward for the wounded and for those returning, while closing against immediate assault. The wolves rotate their wounded inward rather than treating endurance until collapse as holiness.

Wolves first brace, interpose, and contain with shoulder, flank, and body. Two wolves may pin or hold an attacker without tearing. Jaws may carry or hold fur, but fangs must not puncture an attacker. When a hold fails, a wolf may use one controlled shoulder or body impact to throw the immediate attacker far enough to restore protective space. Every throw ends at the boundary: no pursuit, finishing blow, punishment, or seizure of ground follows.

The outward work of the wolves mirrors the Shepherd's inward work:

- As he gathers, they open a path.
- As he separates the endangered from immediate harm, they interpose and hold.
- As he tends wounds, they carry the wounded inward.
- As he receives someone returning, they open the circle without abandoning its protective boundary.

Vaerun is the first wolf shown faltering in this discipline. After throwing an attacker back, he begins to pursue. Another wolf holds him at the boundary. The Shepherd's grief visibly grows; Vaerun sees what his protection was becoming and returns to his place. The restraining wolf need not be identified until drafting. The host remains dangerous and the resistance costly: the wolves bleed, tire, and lose ground without making their restraint a form of effortless superiority.

## D-040 — Little Vey seeks rather than explains

During the ordeal, Little Vey never articulates what he notices. When he perceives what others have missed, he simply seeks Vaerun. At the first return, Vey hears the lost sheep's answering cry because it is too soft for the other wolves to hear, and he smells the remembrance beginning within the sheep. His movement, attention, and choice of whom to seek communicate his perception before any interpretation is given: Vey alerts Vaerun first, and Vaerun's changed attention then alerts the others.

This does not prevent Vey from speaking Vaerun's name in the source-established relational moments or from articulating correction later in his letter. It governs his immediate conduct within the struggle: he does not announce the first return, name the returning sheep, or explain its significance. Because the singular title has not yet accrued during the hill, the active narration names the person he seeks as **Vaerun**, not **the Vaerun**.

## D-041 — Vey's faithfulness to Vaerun is witness

Vey seeks Vaerun because he is faithful to him and trusts his calling. This enacted faithfulness is an essential part of Vey's witness of the Troth: he refuses to abandon Vaerun when perception, violence, fear, or later greatness threatens to isolate him.

Vey's faithfulness does not make Vaerun righteous, infallible, indispensable, or the measure. It is neither flattery nor cultivated dependence. It keeps relationship truthful enough to summon Vaerun back into responsibility and to permit correction. In the ordeal Vey seeks Vaerun bodily without explaining what he has heard; in the later letter he seeks him again through remembrance, warning, and rebuke. These are two forms of the same faithfulness beneath the Shepherd.

## D-042 — The first visible act of return

The first visible act of the lost sheep's return is very small. The sheep hears the Shepherd, begins to remember, and then moves ever so subtly toward him.

The movement is an orientation toward the Shepherd, not a completed repentance, proof of innocence, restored trust, or sufficient repair. Vey has already heard the answering cry, smelled remembrance, and sought Vaerun. Vaerun's attention helps the others begin to perceive the subtle turn. Any later refusal of harm, protection of another, confession, entry into the circle, and repair must grow from this first movement rather than replace it with a dramatic declaration.

## D-043 — Vey smells remembrance

As the lost sheep begins to remember, its scent changes before the return is obvious to the circle. Little Vey smells remembrance. This sensory recognition belongs to the myth's established use of scent to distinguish living identity from imposed near-likeness.

Remembrance here is the beginning of restored relation under the Shepherd's call. It is not nostalgia, recovered innocence, self-absolution, infallible memory, or completed return. Vey does not explain the scent or convert his perception into authority. He seeks Vaerun, continuing the nonverbal and relational witness established in D-040 and D-041.

## D-044 — Do not overuse Little Vey

Preserve all established Little Vey actions and character beats, but do not make him the hidden engine of every turn. During the ordeal he acts chiefly as the quiet support that steadies Vaerun: he perceives what is nearly missed, seeks Vaerun without explanation, remains near him, and calls him back into relation when needed.

Vey is not a universal sensor, secret interpreter, infallible conscience, second savior, or substitute center for the Shepherd. His smallness and faithfulness give him a particular witness, not every necessary action. Other companions retain consequential agency, Vaerun remains responsible for his responses, and the Shepherd remains the center. Limit Vey's speech and appearances so that each carries weight.

## D-045 — The Shepherd heals the lamb during the assault

While the assault against the lamb and flock continues, the Shepherd heals the lamb within the circle. Healing begins before the struggle ends and before morning; it is not postponed until danger has passed. D-062 settles the visible mode as embodied, progressive, and quietly miraculous rather than an instantaneous reset that erases wounds.

The lamb's wounds brought into healing deepen the Shepherd's grief. That grief moves through and strengthens the circle as room, steadiness, renewed capacity to hold, and greater care for the wounded. It does not become magical reinforcement, emotional coercion, combat power, or proof that the wolves are righteous. The wolves mirror the Shepherd's healing work by rotating wounded companions inward, tending them, and making space for recovery while resistance continues.

The lamb remains a creature beneath the Shepherd. Its healing neither identifies it with Christ nor makes its suffering redemptive currency. Healing reveals the Shepherd's active keeping and begins the lamb's restoration to creaturely agency.

## D-046 — The lamb begins to remember what it is

As the Shepherd heals the lamb during the assault, the lamb begins to remember what it is. The assault has wounded and disoriented it but has not created, possessed, or erased its identity. Remembrance begins before its wounds are gone, before the struggle ends, and before the later disclosure of its participation in the Living Lamb.

This remembrance should first become visible through the lamb's attention, posture, and chosen action rather than through explanatory speech. Its later movement from passive protection toward active companionship grows from remembering what it is. The lamb does not remember itself as Christ, savior, sacrifice, or moral measure; it remembers a creaturely identity and vocation received beneath the Shepherd.

Do not conflate the two remembrance movements in the assault. The lost sheep begins to remember the Shepherd and turns toward him; the lamb being healed begins to remember what it is.

## D-047 — Salvation as participation in the Shepherd's proper ordering

In the myth's constructive grammar, **salvation is allowing oneself to participate in the proper ordering of the Shepherd**. The Shepherd's call, judgment, mercy, healing, and opened room come first and make participation possible. Allowing oneself names yielded, non-coerced participation; it does not make consent the source of salvation or turn return into self-rescue.

The proper ordering of the Shepherd is right relation gathered and judged beneath him. It is not submission to Vaerun, the wolves, the circle, an office, or a possible institution. Participation does not certify innocence, erase harm, compel restored trust, or automatically restore a former calling. It begins a life of truth, accountability, repair, companionship, and faithful relation under the Shepherd.

The first returning sheep embodies this order in the following settled sequence:

1. It stops participating in the assault.
2. It refuses the next impulse or command to harm.
3. It opens an escape for a smaller or injured creature.
4. The circle recognizes the turn and opens inward without abandoning its protective boundary.
5. The sheep enters before morning, wounded and accountable but not attacked or made a guardian.
6. Confession begins in these actions; any words come later, remain brief, and do not complete restitution or repair.

Some may follow, some may withdraw, and some may continue the assault. The Shepherd's ordering is offered without being imposed, and morning must not imply universal or compulsory reconciliation.

## D-048 — The returning sheep recognizes the wolves as a sign of order

During its return, the sheep recognizes the wolves as a sign that it is returning to its correctly ordered position beneath the Shepherd. It sees them no longer merely through the Black Sun's disordering accusation, but as fallible guardians holding their proper place around the Shepherd through restraint, protection, and opened room.

The wolves do not cause, certify, or measure the sheep's salvation. They are a sign because their own right relation points beyond them to the Shepherd's ordering. The sheep's correct position is relational rather than hierarchical: beneath the Shepherd, beside other creatures, accountable for harm, and able to receive and contribute to companionship. Recognition does not mean submission to the wolves or immediate trust in every guardian.

D-066 settles the visual beat: after the sheep refuses harm and opens an escape, a smaller creature passes first through the opened circle and the sheep follows without attack. The Shepherd opens rather than removes its curse-sealed eye; it first sees the wolves holding the passage and then the Shepherd for whom they hold it.

## D-049 — The four curses and their embodied marks

Retain the PDF's old mythic name **the four curses**. Call their embodied manifestations **the marks of the curses**, not `signs` or `Vethra`. The provisional graphic is called the `Sigil` under D-054, leaving `mark` available for the marks of the curses and the marks of Vethra without confusion.

The four curses deform four parts of faithful creaturely action:

1. **The fang turned inward:** power deformed into self-destruction, punishment, or violence against what it should protect.
2. **The eye sealed shut:** perception deformed into refusal or inability to see oneself, another, harm, or possible return truthfully.
3. **The paw washed clean of blood:** responsibility deformed into false innocence, erased history, or possessed cleanliness.
4. **The mouth closed around the cry:** witness deformed by silencing, containing, possessing, or refusing the cry and the truthful answer it demands.

The marks are multisensory rather than merely visible. They may have pressure, texture, taste, temperature, vibration, or scent. Their sensory wrongness remains relational and fallible: they deform a creature's familiar scent or texture through something missing, displaced, unnaturally clean, or almost correct. They do not carry one fixed supernatural odor that identifies evil infallibly.

The preferred sensory pattern is:

- the inward fang brings pressure against one's own throat and the taste or smell of iron before a wound appears;
- the sealed eye carries a dry film like ash or wax and makes a world with depth feel falsely smooth;
- the clean paw feels unnaturally smooth and scentless, bearing neither soil, blood, labor, nor kinship;
- the closed mouth holds stale breath and the vibration of a cry trapped behind the teeth.

The curses are imposed deformations, not divine punishments, immutable identities, or proof that someone belongs to the fallen. Their marks may pass over faithful and fallen alike, creating enough near-likeness to provoke distrust. The Gravane preserve their names as warnings so a deformation can be recognized before it finishes its work; naming grants neither immunity nor infallibility.

Little Vey does not become a universal detector of these marks. His smelling of remembrance remains a particular relational perception, not a general power to identify every curse or judge every person.

## D-050 — The White Moon remembers received light

Retain the White Moon from the definitive PDF as reflected, ordinary, creaturely mercy. ## D-053 — Vethra is subtle testimony upon flesh

Vethra is a subtle mark borne upon creaturely flesh, but it is not a scar, wound, badge, curse-mark, institutional Sigil, or visible proof of holiness. It is **a testimony of the Troth upon the flesh through which it has passed**. In the myth's Christological order, this means creaturely flesh bears subtle testimony to participation in the fidelity disclosed and judged by the Shepherd; it does not mean that an autonomous Troth-substance passes through flesh as a second incarnation.

Every companion who passes through the Keeping bears Vethra in a distinct way, whether physically wounded or not, including Harrow after death. Vethra marks them and is borne by them. In the living it may become perceptible through posture, voice, movement, attention, scent, touch, or relation. Harrow's stilled flesh bears it without action or restored breath. Vethra has no uniform shape and cannot be administered, copied, deliberately displayed, or possessed. It belongs to the Shepherd because he is the source, measure, and keeper of the fidelity to which it testifies.

Vethra also testifies to cost truly considered and borne: remaining when departure would be easier, restraining the fang, relinquishing possession, carrying grief, receiving correction, and accepting responsibility. Costliness is not identical with bodily injury, and Vethra is not a receipt proving that enough pain was paid. It is neither reward nor moral wage. Harrow's death does not purchase Vethra, Morning, return, or the strengthening of the circle.

Vethra is subtle and is perceived chiefly, though not exclusively or infallibly, by Gravane and Truvane whose shared remembrance has taught them to notice it. Their recognition is witness rather than judgment. They may overlook, misunderstand, or falsely attribute what they perceive, and Vethra does not prove that its bearer is presently righteous or beyond correction.

Vethra makes a person seem almost `off` in the opposite manner from the fallen near-likenesses. The fallen appear almost right because relation has been imitated, displaced, or removed: a familiar form carries something missing. A bearer of Vethra appears almost strange because ordinary flesh carries a relation and testimony beyond itself: something faithfully received remains present. The contrast must remain relational and difficult to name, not become a supernatural detection system.

In the earthly morning, handle the company as briefly as the initial calling. Establish that Vethra is borne across them all, then use only a few representative embodied details rather than inventorying every companion. Several also carry wounds, but a wound may accompany Vethra and is never identical with it. At least one selected glimpse should involve an unwounded companion so the distinction remains visible.

Harrow remains dead and mourned while his flesh also bears Vethra. This does not preserve or animate his body, make death holy, create an operative presence, certify his righteousness, or enact resurrection before the eschaton. His absence from the living company remains real.

## D-062 — The Shepherd heals through embodied care with quiet miraculous efficacy

The Shepherd's healing of the lamb combines recognizable bodily care with real efficacy beyond ordinary first aid. He supports the lamb so it can breathe, cleans blood and mud from its wounds, tears strips from his already torn robe to bind them, steadies its wounded body with his scarred hands, and continues calling and calming it while the assault persists.

Beneath this care, more healing occurs than the actions alone could naturally accomplish in such conditions. Bleeding slows and stops, breathing steadies, torn flesh begins to close, and strength gradually returns. The narration does not explain a mechanism or stage a spectacle. There is no flash, glow, incantation, staff, or sudden perfect restoration. The Shepherd tends the lamb, and beneath his hands it begins to heal.

Healing remains incomplete during the struggle. The lamb is still weak and marked by what has happened, but it recovers enough life to attend, remember what it is, test its body, and choose movement. Full creaturely restoration is not compressed into this moment, and Morning does not erase the history of the wounds.

The wounds do not transfer to the Shepherd, add new wounds to him, or purchase healing through exchange. His grief deepens because he fully receives the reality of the lamb's suffering while tending it, not because suffering becomes substance, fuel, or redemptive currency. The grief strengthens the circle as room and steadiness under the already settled limits; it does not perform the healing or become combat power.

## D-065 — The Shepherd separates without severing and reorders the deformed member

The Shepherd sorts within bodies, not between persons. He distinguishes a member caught in one of the Black Sun's imposed deformations from the members that remain in right relation, then restores the deformed member to its proper relation with the whole body beneath him. This separation is discernment and reordering, never cutting away, amputation, annihilation, rejection, or classification of the creature as deformed.

The four curses therefore receive corresponding bodily reorderings:

1. The inward fang is drawn away from the throat and returned from punishment or self-destruction toward protection.
2. The sealed eye is opened again to depth, truth, and relation.
3. The falsely clean paw regains creaturely texture, scent, weight, and responsibility without requiring a literal new bloodstain.
4. The mouth closed around the cry is loosened so the cry and truthful answer can pass.

The Shepherd does not discard fang, eye, paw, or mouth. He returns each member to the body and the body to right relation beneath himself. These imposed deformations must remain distinct from wounds, disability, bodily difference, and incomplete healing, none of which indicates sin, lesser worth, or disordered relation.

This establishes the inward and outward mirror: **the Shepherd makes room within bodies; the wolves make room between bodies.** Within the circle, he frees members from postures imposed by the Black Sun so creatures can see, answer, cease harm, bear responsibility, and turn. At the circumference, the wolves restrain harmful movements, separate bodies from immediate assault, and open paths without destroying the bodies they resist. Their work imitates his reordering but neither causes nor completes it.

The Troth is neither a second Logos nor an impersonal law, force, or moral principle above or beside God.

## D-074 — The Black Sun turns true fragments into a false total verdict

The Black Sun may expose true wounds, acts, omissions, fears, betrayals, and misuses of authority. Its falsehood does not require every accusation to be invented. Its deformation lies in isolating a true fragment from relation, proportion, mercy, correction, and possible return, then declaring that fragment the whole and final identity of a person.

The Shepherd neither dismisses truthful exposure nor accepts the Black Sun's totalizing condemnation. He separates what is true from the false final verdict, calls particular failures toward confession and repair, and reopens responsible action without declaring innocence or erasing consequence. The Black Sun remains accusation and deformation, not an infallible revealer or alternate judge beside Christ.

## D-092 — The Keeping Between

The active project name for any order-like form that may be born from this work is **The Keeping Between**.

This is not a replacement name for an already existing body. There is no order yet. The Keeping Between names a vocation produced by a particular historical condition: there are times when keeping requires building, times when keeping requires teaching, and times when the structures themselves are passing away. In such an age, some are called neither to preserve the old world nor to enthrone the new one, but to keep faith through the passage between them.

The Bonhoeffer analogy is formal rather than identical: disciplined Christian formation and witness under concrete historical pressure, without treating the emergent form as permanent, self-justifying, or identical with the Church.

The Keeping Between claims no permanence for itself, possesses no virtue of its own, and does not presume to know what form the world must take. Its charge is smaller and harder:

1. hear what must not be forgotten;
2. guard what must not be surrendered;
3. discern what is being born;
4. remain until the passage is complete.

Then the Keeping may pass away.

**The Troth remains.**

If an order arises, it will be **The Keeping Between**, a provisional order if it is an order at all. The older active phrase **Order of the Troth** is deprecated outside frozen source discussion and historical provenance.

## D-095 — Book of Wolves architecture

The active proposed design for the epistolary workstream is **The Book of Wolves: Letters Concerning the Keeping of the Troth**.

The work is a mythopoetic corpus of letters among wolves, not a systematic theology, catechism, general Christian epistolary collection, Scripture, or ancient pseudepigraphy. It may echo New Testament epistolary form, but within the mythology it remains concrete correspondence concerning the temptations, responsibilities, failures, and forms of faithfulness proper to wolves beneath the Shepherd.

The book asks:

> **How shall a wolf live beneath the Shepherd?**

Its answer must preserve the distinction between wolves and sheep without turning that distinction into dualism. Wolves are not sheep. Wolves are not shepherds. Yet wolves too belong to the Shepherd. Difference of vocation does not imply difference of Lord.

The active twelve-letter form is: Harrow, Sable, Murn, Brindle, Keld, Ash, Rook, Yarrow, Thorn, Ostra, Fen, and Andren. The first eleven letters are written by **Andren**, the Book-of-Wolves personal name for the wolf who bears the office of Vaerun. The twelfth is written by **Little Vey to Andren**, reversing the direction of correspondence and correcting Andren's danger of identifying himself with the office.

The Andren naming belongs to the Book of Wolves correspondence workstream and does not revise the accepted *Aru Va'en* narration, where the narrator's personal name remains unstated.

The opening and closing bookends are:

> **Harrow, you are not your vow.**

and:

> **Andren, you are not the Vaerun.**

Little Vey writes the prologue near the end of his life, preserving the correspondence and possibly naming the collection. He should not receive an instructional letter from Andren. His final corrective letter gives Andren his name back and remembers Andren's own doctrine back to him.
