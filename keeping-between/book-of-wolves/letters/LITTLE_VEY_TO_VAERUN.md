# The Letter of Little Vey to the Vaerun
## Concerning Remembrance as Stature Increases

**Status:** `working_synthesis`

This active revision preserves the recovered source letter at `source/book-of-the-troth/The Letter of Little Vey to the Vaerun.md` unchanged. It develops the user-established summons to remember where the companions stood, where the Shepherd stood, and how the Troth must remain in memory as a Vaerun becomes known among the many as the Vaerun.

**Little Vey, keeper of the ember and companion in the Troth,**

**to the one whom many now call the Vaerun, my beloved companion, who stood beside me as a Vaerun before his keeping became known among many, and to all who stand near him and call him faithful:**

Grace to you beneath the Shepherd, and peace among all who keep without possessing.

**Before throne and pasture.**

**Before fang and fleece.**

**The Troth remains!**

### Remembrance

I give thanks when I remember the road we have walked together, because I knew you before many spoke your name.

I remember when you did not know whether you could remain.

I remember the trembling beneath your strength.

I remember when you stood because there was nowhere else to stand, and not because anyone was watching.

You were a Vaerun then.

Only we knew your name.

Now the many call you the Vaerun.

You did not ask whether your keeping would be remembered.

There was a cry.

You heard it.

And you remained.

For this I give thanks to the Shepherd, who places keepers where they are needed and who does not measure them according to their stature.

Remember these things, brother.

For others now remember you differently.

### Remember Where We Stood

Remember where we stood, Vaerun.

Remember when only we knew your name.

Remember where the Shepherd stood.

Before the hill, before we saw where the Shepherd stood, you were a Vaerun among others who had crossed and might yet cross. After the hill, the many began to call you the Vaerun.

The hill did not make you a higher kind of keeper.

The Shepherd did not set you above your companions.

The greatness came later, in the mouths of those who told where you had stood.

We stood paw beside paw, each keeping the place beside him because none of us could keep the whole. We stood in mud and blood, near enough to hear one another breathe, with the cry before us and no greatness in us that could answer it alone.

The Shepherd stood at the center.

He did not stand above us as the reward of our keeping. He stood among the wounded, holding what we could not save, making room where fear and violence had closed every path.

We did not make that center.

We found ourselves kept within it.

This is how I ask you to remember the Troth: not as the greatness of the one who remained, but as the fidelity that called us out of ourselves and held us beneath the Shepherd.

As you become great among others, return to that place in memory.

Remember where we stood.

Remember where the Shepherd stood.

### Concerning What I Have Heard

Word has reached even the small places concerning you.

They speak of the Vaerun as though the words named more than the calling you bore.

They have taken a calling many may bear and made it a title only you may answer.

They repeat your words.

They tell stories of where you stood and of those whom you kept.

They speak less often of where we stood.

Less often still of where the Shepherd stood.

Some seek your judgment before they have listened for the cry themselves.

Some speak your name when they mean faithfulness.

Some have begun to look toward you when they should look beyond you.

I do not write this because you have asked them to do these things.

That is why I write.

There are thrones built by those who sit upon them.

There are also thrones built beneath a man while he is still standing.

Take care that you do not discover too late which kind has been raised beneath you.

### The Danger of Stature

You have grown in the sight of others.

Do not therefore grow in your own sight.

As your greatness increases among others, let your remembrance of the Troth increase more quickly. Let every praise return you to the ground where no one stood alone and where the Shepherd, not the Vaerun, held the center.

You did not cross the veil in order to become the Vaerun. The many made that title after the hill. Do not let their telling persuade you that the title was the meaning of the crossing.

For stature is a strange temptation to the keeper.

The faithless may desire power openly, and therefore their hunger can sometimes be seen.

But the faithful are tempted more subtly.

They are praised for remaining.

Then they are trusted because they remained.

Then they are obeyed because they are trusted.

Then their judgments become difficult to question because once, long ago, they remained when others fled.

And at last the keeper may come to believe that because he has kept faithfully, he knows what ought to be kept.

This is how a threshold becomes a throne.

You must not permit it.

Your keeping was not entrusted to you because you are greater than those beside you.

Nor does the Troth remain because the Vaerun remains.

If tomorrow your strength failed completely, **The Troth would remain.**

If your name were forgotten, **The Troth would remain.**

If every tale concerning your faithfulness were discovered to contain error, weakness, and failure, still:

**The Troth would remain.**

Take comfort in this.

And fear it also.

For you are not required to hold together that which the Shepherd alone can hold.

### Concerning the Little Ones

Perhaps you wonder why this warning comes from me.

It comes because I am small.

There are things visible from below that become difficult to see from above.

When many gather around a great keeper, those nearest his feet begin to disappear from his sight.

Not because he despises them.

Simply because stature changes what can be seen.

I have stood in places where you could not stand.

Not because I was more faithful.

Because I was little enough to fit there.

There are cries that do not reach the high places.

One ear has heard poorly since the hill. Do not therefore trust me as though I hear every cry. Keep me near, and keep others nearer.

There are frightened ones who will speak to neither wolf nor guardian, however gentle the guardian intends to be.

There are wounds that recoil from strength.

There are embers that a great hand may scatter while trying to protect them.

Do not therefore surround yourself only with those who stand at your height.

Keep little ones near you.

Listen when they tell you what they can see beneath your reach.

And never mistake their smallness for lesser discernment.

The Shepherd has need of wolves.

The Shepherd also has need of creatures who can enter places where wolves cannot.

Neither should boast against the other.

### Do Not Become the Measure

I beg you especially concerning this:

**Do not allow yourself to become the measure of the Troth.**

There will be those who ask, “What would the Vaerun do?”

Teach them instead to ask:

**“What cry have I trained myself not to hear?”**

There will be those who say, “The Vaerun has judged this faithful.”

Tell them that your judgment may fail.

There will be those who wish to imitate your manner, your speech, your keeping, perhaps even your wounds.

Do not permit them to mistake resemblance to you for resemblance to Christ.

The keeper is a witness.

The keeper is not the Logos.

The guardian points beyond himself or eventually becomes an idol standing in the doorway he was appointed to guard.

Better that they forget your name and learn mercy than remember every saying attributed to you and cease to hear the wounded.

Better that any fellowship gathered around your name diminish than that it possess what was only entrusted to it.

Better that your authority become smaller than that the Shepherd become smaller in the imagination of those who follow you.

For we do not possess the Troth.

We are kept beneath the Shepherd in the fidelity the Troth names.

### Remember That You Must Be Kept

There is another danger in stature.

When others begin to believe you are strong, they may stop asking whether you are tired.

When they call you guardian, they may forget that guardians bleed.

When they call you the Vaerun, they may cease to remember the companion who once wondered whether he could remain.

Do not help them forget him.

He is still within you.

Let him speak.

When you are tired, say that you are tired.

When you do not know, say that you do not know.

When you have judged wrongly, repent without defending the stature others have given you.

When another must correct you, make room for him.

And when you can no longer keep, allow yourself to be kept.

You once taught many that nothing faithful keeps alone.

See that this saying does not become something you teach others but refuse for yourself.

The keeper who cannot be kept has already begun to leave the Troth while imagining himself its greatest servant.

### Concerning Unclaimed Virtue

Do not call yourself humble because you receive this warning.

Do not call yourself faithful because you obey it.

Do not even make your refusal of greatness into another greatness.

This temptation has many doors.

A man may boast of his throne.

Another may boast that he refused one.

Both may still be looking at themselves.

We have no virtue that requires the world to recognize us as virtuous.

Therefore receive whatever stature has come to you as something dangerous and temporary.

Use whatever authority has been entrusted to you for the one who cries.

Then release it.

Keep without possessing.

Teach without becoming the teaching.

Lead without requiring anyone to become your follower.

Stand without demanding that your standing be remembered.

And should another become better fitted for the place where you stand, rejoice and move aside.

The Troth will not diminish.

### An Exhortation to the Vaerun

So I charge you, brother:

Remain near the ground.

Keep company with those who cannot increase your stature.

Hear those who are difficult to hear.

Make room for contradiction.

Permit yourself to be corrected by those with no title.

Never allow gratitude to become obedience owed to you.

Never permit suffering endured in the past to become authority over another in the present.

Do not confuse being needed with being indispensable.

Do not confuse being trusted with being right.

Do not confuse being followed with knowing the road.

And above all:

**Never let those whom you have kept believe that you are the reason they were kept.**

You are a Vaerun.

That is a vocation.

They call you the Vaerun.

That is stature others have given you.

Neither is a throne.

The day may come when the most faithful thing the Vaerun can do is become small enough to disappear from the place where everyone expects him to stand.

If that day comes, do not fear it.

The Shepherd loses nothing when a servant becomes small.

### The Word of the Tiny Keeper

I have little authority with which to command you.

That may be why I dare to write.

I have only what was given to me:

a small place,

a little ember,

and the Troth remembered from where we stood.

So hear this word from the tiny keeper:

There are places where the great cannot stand.

There are cries stature cannot hear.

There are wounds strength cannot touch without causing further pain.

And there are times when the guardian himself must become small enough to be held.

Should your stature continue to grow, then let your claim upon it become smaller.

Should your voice carry farther, listen more carefully for the quiet voice beneath it.

Should others raise you higher, remember where we stood.

Should the many speak your name, remember when only we knew it.

Should they call you great, remember where the Shepherd stood.

Should they remember only your keeping, remember the Troth that kept no one alone.

And should they ever build a throne beneath you—

step down.

Come sit beside me.

We will keep the ember together.

### Blessing

May the Shepherd preserve you from the temptation to become necessary.

May Christ remain greater in your sight as you become greater in the sight of others.

May remembrance of the Troth deepen whenever others magnify your name.

May the Spirit trouble you whenever praise becomes possession.

May the small continue to speak where you can hear them.

May those you keep also keep you.

And when your strength has ended, may you discover without shame that you too have always been carried.

I remain your companion beneath the Shepherd,

**Little Vey**

keeper of no throne,

guardian of no greatness,

tender of the ember.

**Before throne and pasture.**

**Before fang and fleece.**

**The Troth remains!**
