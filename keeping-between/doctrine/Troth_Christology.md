# Troth Christology — Governing Constructive Note

## The problem

The word *Troth* is powerful precisely because it sounds primordial. That creates a theological risk: the Troth can accidentally become an abstract principle that stands beside or above Christ.

The project must refuse that move.

## Accepted governing relation

Under D-073, the accepted constructive formulation is:

> **The Troth names the fidelity of God disclosed and enacted in Jesus Christ, and the creaturely calling to participate in that fidelity by the Spirit.**

This preserves the objectivity of the Troth while refusing a second Logos. It is constructive theology, not the lexical meaning of any biblical word and not a claim that the project's mythic language is Scripture.

## Important internal control

The myth itself develops a further correction: the Shepherd is prior to the Troth. This suggests that covenant, fidelity, or keeping is not self-originating. The personal divine source precedes the vow's creaturely recognition.

That should produce an ordering such as:

**Christ / Shepherd → fidelity / Troth → creaturely keeping → fellowship / office / liturgy**

not:

**Troth → Christ as example of Troth.**

## Required continuing review

Continue testing the formulation and its uses against:
- John 1;
- John 10;
- John 15 and Johannine `menō`/abiding;
- Philippians 2;
- 1 John 4;
- biblical covenant faithfulness;
- Pauline `pistis`/faithfulness debates;
- theological accounts of Christ as Logos, truth, and the form of divine self-giving.

Do not claim Greek lexical equivalence between *Troth* and any single NT term.
