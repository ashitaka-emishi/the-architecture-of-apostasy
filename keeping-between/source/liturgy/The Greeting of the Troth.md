# The Greeting of the Troth

## I. The Apostolic Greeting

When two who keep the Troth meet, one may offer the first word:

**First:**  
*Before throne and pasture.*

The other shall answer:

**Second:**  
*Before fang and fleece.*

And together they shall say:

**Both:**  
***The Troth remains!***

No rank determines who gives the first word.

The elder may speak first to the novice, or the novice to the elder.  
The commander may speak first to the servant, or the servant to the commander.  
The stranger may speak first to the known, and the returning may speak first to those who remained.

For throne is named precisely so that throne shall not govern the greeting.

The Greeting is not a declaration that either speaker possesses the Troth. Neither says, *I am faithful*, nor *I am righteous*, nor *I bear the sign.*

They confess only that the Troth precedes them and survives them.

### When the Greeting Is Used

The Greeting may be exchanged:

- upon meeting after absence;
- when entering or departing from a common work;
- before deliberation in which authority, loyalty, fear, or self-interest may obscure judgment;
- between persons of unequal station, as a reminder that both answer to that which precedes rank;
- when receiving one who comes peacefully from outside the fellowship;
- before an undertaking in which courage, sacrifice, or obedience will be demanded;
- after reconciliation, when fellowship has been restored but the memory of failure is not erased;
- at the opening of a gathering of the Host, when a full liturgical office is not required.

It should not become a casual password or badge of membership.

To speak it is to submit oneself to its judgment.

### Meaning of the First Word

**Before throne and pasture.**

The throne signifies power, rule, office, command, law, and every structure by which one person governs another.

The pasture signifies belonging, security, peoplehood, fellowship, homeland, congregation, and every enclosure within which a people calls itself *us*.

Neither is condemned simply for existing.

Neither is permitted to become ultimate.

There was Troth before there was throne.

There was Troth before anyone drew a boundary around the pasture and called those within it *our own*.

Therefore no ruler may make falsehood true by commanding it, and no community may make evil good by embracing it together.

### Meaning of the Answer

**Before fang and fleece.**

The fang signifies the power to wound: aggression, domination, hatred, vengeance, predation, and the terrible possibility that a human being may become wolf to another.

The fleece signifies vulnerability and innocence, but also the identity of the victim, the protected, the righteous, and those who believe themselves safely numbered among the sheep.

The Troth precedes both.

Therefore the wolf cannot declare his violence good merely because he believes himself necessary.

And the sheep cannot declare itself righteous merely because it has suffered.

Neither guilt nor innocence possesses the Troth.

Both stand beneath it.

### Meaning of the Common Acclamation

**The Troth remains!**

This line belongs to neither speaker separately.

They speak it together because neither possesses what is confessed.

Kingdoms fail.

Pastures burn.

Wolves repent.

Sheep become wolves.

The faithful betray.

The fallen return.

Names are forgotten.

Orders become corrupt.

Signs are counterfeited.

Virtues become masks.

Still:

**The Troth remains.**

The acclamation is therefore simultaneously confession, warning, hope, and promise.

It warns the powerful that the Troth survives their authority.

It warns the community that the Troth survives its consensus.

It warns the righteous that the Troth survives their righteousness.

It tells the fallen that the Troth survives their fall.

And because it remains, return remains possible.

# Companion Exchanges

These forms do not replace the Apostolic Greeting. They grow from it and are used at particular moments of the Path.

## II. The Greeting of Departure

Used when companions separate for a dangerous journey, difficult duty, or uncertain absence.

**One:**  
*Though the road divide us.*

**Other:**  
*Though the night conceal us.*

**Both:**  
***The Troth remains!***

The exchange does not promise that both will return. It promises only that distance, darkness, and uncertainty do not alter that to which both remain accountable.

---

## III. The Greeting of Mourning

Used before the dead, among the bereaved, or when naming a loss that cannot be repaired.

**One:**  
*The voice is silent.*

**Other:**  
*The place stands empty.*

**Both:**  
***The Troth remains.***

Here the final words are spoken without triumph.

The Troth remaining does not mean that death is unreal or that grief should cease. It means that death does not retroactively make love meaningless, nor does loss annul the obligations of the living.

The dead are not made useful by their deaths.

They are remembered because they were persons.

---

## IV. The Greeting of Repentance

Used when one who has failed, betrayed, fled, harmed another, or become wolf seeks return.

**The Returning:**  
*I did not remain.*

**The Keeper:**  
*You are not the measure.*

**Both:**  
***The Troth remains.***

This exchange should never function as automatic absolution.

The first speaker confesses failure without euphemism.

The second refuses both condemnation and flattery. The returning person is neither permitted to redefine the Troth around the failure nor told that the failure never mattered.

The possibility of repentance exists precisely because the Troth did not fall when the person fell.

After this exchange come confession, restitution where possible, judgment where necessary, and the work of return.

---

## V. The Greeting of Reconciliation

Used when two who were divided have truthfully named the injury between them and choose again to walk in fellowship.

**One:**  
*What passed between us is known.*

**Other:**  
*What was broken is not forgotten.*

**Both:**  
***Yet the Troth remains.***

Reconciliation does not require the falsification of memory.

The wound is not renamed harmless.

The wrong is not made righteous.

The past is not erased so that peace may appear easier than it is.

They proceed together because truth has been faced, not because truth has been suppressed.

---

## VI. The Greeting Before Judgment

Used before councils, disciplinary hearings, command decisions, or any occasion when one person or body must exercise authority over another.

**One:**  
*No throne stands above it.*

**Other:**  
*No fleece stands beyond it.*

**Both:**  
***The Troth remains.***

This exchange reminds the judge that authority does not confer righteousness and reminds the accused that suffering does not automatically confer innocence.

Both judgment and mercy must therefore answer to something neither party controls.

---

# The Root Form

All these exchanges return to the first:

**First:**  
*Before throne and pasture.*

**Second:**  
*Before fang and fleece.*

**Both:**  
***The Troth remains!***

This is the ordinary greeting because it contains the others within it.

Before power.  
Before belonging.  
Before guilt.  
Before innocence.

Before what we rule.  
Before whom we call ours.  
Before what we have done.  
Before what has been done to us.

**The Troth remains.**
