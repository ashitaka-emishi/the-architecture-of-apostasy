The primary relationship between the Greek word ginosko (γινωσκω) and the Hebrew word da'at (δα'ατ / דַּעַת) is that they serve as linguistic counterparts in biblical literature to express experiential, relational, and intimate knowledge rather than mere intellectual information.

While classical Greek philosophy treated knowledge as an abstract, intellectual concept, New Testament writers heavily infused *ginosko* with the holistic, deeply personal meaning of *da'at*. 

The Septuagint Connection (The Linguistic Bridge)

The direct link between these two concepts was forged in the [Septuagint](https://wsumc.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/yada-ginosko.pdf), the 3rd-century BCE Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible. 

* The Root Words: *Da'at* is the Hebrew noun for "knowledge," derived from the root verb *yada* (to know).   
* The Translation: Whenever the Jewish translators encountered *yada* or *da'at* in the Hebrew text, they overwhelmingly chose the Greek verb *ginosko* and its noun form, *gnosis*, to translate them into Greek.  
* The Result: This translation practice permanently forced the Greek vocabulary to carry the heavy theological and relational weight of Hebrew thought. 

Core Conceptual Commonalities

When these words intersect in biblical theology, they share several distinct characteristics:

| Feature  | Hebrew *Da'at* / *Yada* | Greek *Ginosko* |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Primary Source | Acquired via direct, personal experience. | Acquired via observation and relationship. |
| Intellectual vs. Experiential | Subverts pure theory; prioritizes active relationship. | Shifts in biblical usage from abstract logic to relational familiarity. |
| Ultimate Intimacy | Famously used to describe the covenantal intimacy of marriage (e.g., Genesis 4:1). | Used in the same intimate sense in the New Testament (e.g., Luke 1:34). |

Impact on the New Testament

Because of the influence of *da'at*, when New Testament writers used *ginosko*, they were rarely talking about passing a written test or memorizing data. 

For example, in [John 17:3](https://www.facebook.com/groups/religionfreemovement/posts/875102752941560/), Jesus states that eternal life is to "know (ginosko) the only true God." Because of the word's relationship to *da'at*, this does not mean knowing facts *about* God. It means being in an active, deeply personal, and transformative covenant relationship with Him.
