# Pastoral Risk Review

Use this checklist before publishing or canonically integrating work that deals
with trauma, addiction, abuse, church wounds, institutional betrayal,
psychological formation, despair, self-harm, coercion, or spiritual authority.

The goal is not to soften every strong claim. The goal is to make sure the work
tells the truth without shaming the afflicted, excusing harm, pretending to offer
clinical care, or trapping the reader in accusation without a path of return.

## 1. Trigger

- [ ] The piece discusses trauma, abuse, neglect, addiction, compulsion, or
  psychological formation.
- [ ] The piece critiques pastors, churches, ministries, institutions, or
  authority structures.
- [ ] The piece uses intense language around bondage, disease, possession,
  despair, violation, sacrifice, or corruption.
- [ ] The piece gives pastoral counsel or may be read as pastoral counsel.
- [ ] The piece names victims, perpetrators, communities, or identifiable
  institutions.
- [ ] The piece could be read by someone in crisis, active addiction, active
  abuse, grief, or spiritual disorientation.

If any box is checked, complete the rest of this review.

## 2. Claim Classification

- [ ] Identify which claims are theological interpretation.
- [ ] Identify which claims are biblical exegesis.
- [ ] Identify which claims are historical, legal, medical, psychological, or
  sociological claims.
- [ ] Mark claims that require visible citations.
- [ ] Mark claims that require rival readings or tradition-specific caveats.
- [ ] Mark claims that should be reframed as analogy, pastoral observation, or
  speculative synthesis rather than stated as settled fact.

## 3. Reader Safety

- [ ] The piece does not blame victims for their wounds, symptoms, silence, or
  delayed disclosure.
- [ ] The piece does not imply that addiction, trauma, depression, anxiety,
  compulsive behavior, or abuse recovery is solved by willpower alone.
- [ ] The piece does not use demonic, diseased, polluted, or enslaved language in
  a way that collapses the afflicted person into the affliction.
- [ ] The piece distinguishes sin, suffering, sickness, formation, coercion, and
  culpability where that distinction matters.
- [ ] The piece avoids graphic detail unless the detail is necessary for the
  argument and handled with restraint.
- [ ] The piece does not instruct a reader to remain in danger, secrecy, or
  unaccountable spiritual control.

## 4. Pastoral And Clinical Boundaries

- [ ] The piece does not present itself as medical, psychiatric, legal, or
  emergency advice.
- [ ] Any clinical or diagnostic language is qualified and sourced.
- [ ] Any advice about abuse, self-harm, addiction treatment, or mental health
  directs the reader toward qualified local help where appropriate.
- [ ] The piece avoids promising outcomes that pastoral writing cannot guarantee.
- [ ] The piece does not use theological language to replace medical care,
  therapy, mandated reporting, civil authority, or practical safety planning.
- [ ] The piece names the limits of its counsel when a reader's situation could
  involve immediate danger.

## 5. Institutional And Authority Risk

- [ ] Critique of institutions names concrete risks rather than making totalizing
  claims about all churches, pastors, traditions, or structures.
- [ ] The piece distinguishes faithful authority from coercive or self-protective
  authority.
- [ ] The piece does not excuse institutional silence, retaliation, reputation
  defense, financial opacity, ignored complaints, or discipline without
  restoration.
- [ ] The piece does not treat a board, polity document, title, ordination, or
  public ministry as proof that accountability is real.
- [ ] When accusations or identifiable cases are discussed, the piece uses
  verified sources, avoids unnecessary naming, and does not speculate beyond the
  record.
- [ ] The piece offers a path toward truthful accountability, repair, protection,
  or lament rather than only contempt.

## 6. Theological Integrity

- [ ] The created good is named before its corruption is exposed.
- [ ] The wounded person remains an image-bearer, not an example, symbol, or
  object lesson only.
- [ ] The critique resolves toward Christ, repentance, protection, healing, and
  restoration rather than despair or spectacle.
- [ ] The Anti-Nehushtan rule is preserved: symbols, institutions, tools, and
  pastoral forms remain servants and signs, not objects of trust in themselves.
- [ ] Scapegoat analysis does not create a new scapegoat.
- [ ] The piece leaves room for lament, unresolved suffering, and slow repair.

## 7. Revision Decision

- [ ] Ready for publication or integration.
- [ ] Ready after minor pastoral cautions are added.
- [ ] Needs citations or rival readings before publication.
- [ ] Needs theological revision before publication.
- [ ] Needs clinical, legal, safeguarding, or pastoral consultation before
  publication.
- [ ] Hold; the current form creates avoidable pastoral risk.
