# Ruth and Remaining

Ruth begins where faithfulness becomes difficult: with loss.

Naomi has lost her husband and sons. Ruth has lost her husband and whatever future she expected. Naomi releases Ruth and Orpah from obligation. Orpah returns home.

Ruth remains.

> "Where you go, I will go;
> where you lodge, I will lodge;
> your people shall be my people,
> and your God my God."
> - Ruth 1:16

Ruth does not know what remaining will bring. She does not know about Boaz, Obed, David, or her eventual place in the genealogy of Christ.

She only knows that Naomi is going home.

And she goes with her.

This is what makes Ruth such a strong image of the Troth. Her faithfulness is not transactional. She does not remain because she has been promised reward. Naomi has almost nothing left to give her.

Ruth remains because Naomi is still worth keeping.

When Naomi reaches Bethlehem, she calls herself bitter and says she has returned empty, even though Ruth stands beside her. Ruth does not demand recognition. She does not correct Naomi's grief.

She remains.

Then remaining becomes labor. Ruth goes into the fields and gleans grain. Her promise takes bodily form. Her feet followed Naomi; now her hands feed her.

The Troth cannot remain only in speech. It must become action.

Boaz eventually recognizes what Ruth has done and begins keeping the keeper. He protects her, feeds her, and ensures that she can gather enough grain.

Ruth therefore teaches another principle:

Nothing faithful keeps alone.

The keeper must also be kept.

Ruth never claims her fidelity as proof of her own virtue. She does not call herself righteous or superior. Others recognize her faithfulness while she simply continues the work before her.

Her virtue remains unclaimed.

And redemption begins there.

Boaz later becomes the kinsman-redeemer, but the movement toward restoration began earlier, on the road from Moab, when Ruth refused to let Naomi walk alone.

Ruth cannot undo Naomi's losses.

She cannot resurrect the dead.

She cannot promise that everything will become well.

She can only remain.

Sometimes redemption begins exactly there: when abandonment is interrupted.

Ruth is therefore not primarily the warrior, prophet, priest, or ruler.

She is the one who remains.

Before guardianship comes remaining.

Before one keeps the flock, one learns to keep faith with one suffering person.

Before title, office, mark, or Order, there is simply this:

The world has broken something.  
The future has made no promise.  
The road is long.  
Still, I remain.

That is Ruth.

And there the Troth remains.
