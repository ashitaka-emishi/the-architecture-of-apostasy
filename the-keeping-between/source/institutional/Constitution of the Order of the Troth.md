# Constitution of the Order of the Troth
## Draft 0.1

### Preamble

The Order of the Troth is a Christian order of guardianship formed under the lordship of Jesus Christ, the Shepherd.

The Order exists to form and sustain persons called to keep what has been entrusted to them without possessing it; to recognize and resist the processes by which persons, institutions, offices, and communities become enthroned; and to practice faithful guardianship under mutual accountability.

The Order is not the Church.

The Order does not possess the Troth.

The Order has no claim to perpetuity.

Its institutions exist only so long as they serve its calling faithfully.

**Even the keeper must be kept.  
Nothing faithful keeps alone.**

---

# Article I — Name and Purpose

The name of this body shall be **The Order of the Troth**.

Its purposes are:

1. to form persons in the Rule of the Troth;
2. to cultivate discernment concerning individual and institutional apostasy;
3. to prepare guardians for service within churches, institutions, communities, and other places of responsibility;
4. to provide mutual keeping and accountability among guardians;
5. to develop and preserve the theological tradition associated with the Architecture of Apostasy;
6. to serve rather than govern the wider Church;
7. to bear witness to the lordship of Christ over every human throne.

The Order shall not exist primarily for its own expansion, prestige, wealth, or preservation.

---

# Article II — Authority

Jesus Christ is Lord of the Order.

Holy Scripture is the Order's primary canonical witness to Christ and stands above the Rule, Constitution, teachings, traditions, offices, and decisions of the Order.

The **Rule of the Troth** governs the vocation and conduct of members.

This Constitution governs the institutional life of the Order.

The theological writings of the Architecture of Apostasy tradition may instruct the Order but shall not possess canonical authority.

No founder, officer, council, teacher, or text of the Order is infallible.

---

# Article III — Membership

Membership shall be voluntary.

No person shall be born into membership or considered a member merely through family, association, attendance, employment, or participation in Order activities.

A person enters the Order through:

1. a period of inquiry;
2. study of the Rule;
3. formation under the guidance of members;
4. voluntary profession of the Keeper's Promise.

Membership in the Order does not supersede membership in a Christian church or communion.

A member may leave the Order freely.

Departure alone shall never constitute apostasy, betrayal, or misconduct.

---

# Article IV — The Guardianships

The Order recognizes three forms of guardianship:

## Vaelun

The Vaelun is formed in threshold guardianship, discernment, listening, and recognition of exclusion, distortion, and emerging apostasy.

## Vaerun

The Vaerun assumes sustained responsibility for keeping persons, communities, teachings, institutions, or other entrusted goods without possessing them.

## Hrovann

The Hrovann is entrusted with guardianship near concentrated authority and with particular responsibility for preventing the sacralization and corruption of institutional power.

These guardianships shall not constitute degrees of human worth, spiritual superiority, or privilege.

Advancement shall mean increased responsibility and increased accountability.

No guardianship shall be permanent merely by virtue of having once been received.

A member may return to a less demanding form of guardianship without disgrace.

---

# Article V — Governance

The Order shall be governed through distributed councils rather than a single sovereign office.

No individual shall possess unilateral authority over doctrine, membership, discipline, finances, appointments, and constitutional interpretation simultaneously.

Governing bodies shall include representation from multiple guardianships.

Authority shall be delegated for specific purposes and shall remain subject to review.

Every exercise of significant authority shall answer three questions:

**What has been entrusted?**

**For what purpose has authority been given?**

**Who keeps the keeper exercising it?**

---

# Article VI — The Council of Keeping

The principal governing body shall be the **Council of Keeping**.

Its responsibilities shall include:

1. stewardship of the Rule and Constitution;
2. oversight of formation;
3. recognition of guardianships;
4. financial stewardship;
5. protection of members and communities;
6. institutional self-examination;
7. maintenance of records;
8. convening assemblies of the Order.

No member of the Council shall serve indefinitely.

Terms shall be limited and staggered.

The Council shall not possess authority to declare itself the exclusive interpreter of the Troth.

---

# Article VII — The Hrovann and the Throne

No office within the Order shall constitute a throne immune from challenge.

Where the Order establishes executive leadership, that leadership shall remain accountable to an independent body capable of investigation, correction, suspension, and removal.

Hrovann serving in institutional leadership shall possess a particular duty to identify:

- concentration of authority;
- concealment of harm;
- suppression of dissent;
- institutional self-preservation;
- personality cults;
- claims of exceptional necessity;
- misuse of sacred language to protect leadership.

The Hrovann shall not become the throne they are appointed to guard.

---

# Article VIII — Mutual Keeping

Every member shall have access to relationships of accountability outside their immediate chain of authority.

No leader shall select all persons responsible for that leader's oversight.

No accusation of misconduct shall be judged solely by persons subordinate to the accused.

Members may raise concerns without first obtaining permission from the person or body being challenged.

Good-faith criticism shall not constitute disloyalty.

Retaliation against those who raise good-faith concerns shall itself constitute serious misconduct.

---

# Article IX — Institutional Examination

At regular intervals, the Order shall formally examine its own life according to the Architecture of Apostasy.

The examination shall ask whether:

- mission has become identity;
- identity has become possession;
- possession has produced self-preservation;
- self-preservation has demanded sacrifice;
- sacrifice has been justified through a false account of necessity;
- suffering has been hidden to preserve the Order's self-image;
- leaders have become symbolic embodiments of the Order;
- criticism has become equivalent to betrayal;
- institutional survival has become an ultimate good.

The findings of this examination shall be available to the membership.

The Order shall not investigate apostasy everywhere except within itself.

---

# Article X — Discipline

Discipline shall exist for truth, protection, correction, and where possible restoration.

It shall never exist primarily to protect reputation.

Processes shall distinguish among:

- error;
- disagreement;
- negligence;
- abuse of authority;
- deliberate misconduct;
- persistent violation of the Rule.

No member shall be disciplined merely for criticizing leadership, questioning interpretation, or proposing reform.

Accused persons shall receive fair process.

Those reporting harm shall receive protection from retaliation.

Forgiveness shall not require restoration to office.

Reconciliation shall not require renewed vulnerability to abuse.

---

# Article XI — Financial Stewardship

The property and finances of the Order belong to its mission and not to its officers.

Financial records shall be subject to independent review.

No single individual shall control authorization, expenditure, and review of significant funds.

Compensation shall be transparent to the appropriate governing bodies.

Donations shall not purchase doctrinal, disciplinary, or governing authority.

The Order shall accumulate resources only insofar as those resources serve its calling.

Institutional wealth shall never be treated as evidence of divine favor.

---

# Article XII — Teaching and Doctrine

The Order may develop theological positions and teaching materials.

A distinction shall be maintained among:

**Scripture** — canonical authority;

**Rule** — vowed discipline;

**Constitution** — institutional governance;

**doctrine** — theological conclusions of the Order;

**commentary** — interpretation and scholarship;

**myth and literature** — formative works such as *Aru Va'en*.

These categories shall not be collapsed.

A teaching may be revised without threatening the existence of the Order.

Disagreement with nonessential interpretations shall not by itself exclude a member.

---

# Article XIII — Amendment

This Constitution may be amended.

No generation possesses authority to prevent future generations from correcting its institutional arrangements.

Amendment shall require:

1. publication of the proposed change;
2. a period of reflection and response;
3. consideration by more than one governing body;
4. a substantial supermajority of the appropriate representative assembly.

Articles protecting accountability, voluntary departure, institutional examination, and the subordinate status of the Order shall require a higher threshold for amendment.

---

# Article XIV — Succession and Founders

Founders shall possess no permanent constitutional privilege.

No office shall pass through family inheritance.

No founder may appoint an unrestricted successor.

Historical contribution shall be honored without becoming institutional sovereignty.

After the death, departure, or retirement of a founder, the Order shall remain free to revise the founder's interpretations and decisions.

Loyalty to a founder is not loyalty to the Troth.

---

# Article XV — Schism and Peaceful Separation

Disagreement may sometimes make continued common governance impossible.

The Order shall therefore establish procedures for peaceful separation.

Those who separate shall not automatically be declared enemies, apostates, or illegitimate.

Property disputes shall be resolved according to previously established procedures rather than theological condemnation.

No faction shall claim that possession of the Order's name proves possession of the Troth.

---

# Article XVI — Dissolution

The Order may dissolve itself.

Dissolution shall be considered when the Order can no longer fulfill its calling without violating its Rule.

Upon dissolution, remaining assets shall be transferred to charitable, educational, ecclesial, or related purposes consistent with the Order's mission and shall not be distributed among members.

No officer shall possess authority to prevent dissolution solely to preserve office, employment, reputation, or institutional continuity.

The death of the Order does not constitute the death of the Troth.

---

# Article XVII — Constitutional Principle

Where this Constitution is ambiguous, interpretation shall favor:

accountability over concentrated authority;

truth over institutional reputation;

persons over institutional preservation;

restoration over vengeance;

protection over concealment;

voluntary fidelity over compelled loyalty;

and the lordship of Christ over the preservation of the Order.

No constitutional provision shall be interpreted to mean that the Order must survive.

For the Order itself is only a keeper.

**Even the keeper must be kept.  
Nothing faithful keeps alone.**

**The Troth remains.**
