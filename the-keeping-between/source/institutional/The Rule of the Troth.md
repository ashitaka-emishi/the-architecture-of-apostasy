# The Rule of the Troth
## A Rule for Those Who Keep Without Possessing

### Preamble

Before throne and pasture.  
Before fang and fleece.  
The Troth remains.

We undertake this Rule not because we claim virtue, but because we know how easily keeping becomes possession, service becomes dominion, conviction becomes idolatry, and guardians become what they were appointed to resist.

Christ alone is Lord.

The Shepherd alone possesses the flock.

No office, institution, tradition, nation, church, order, guardian, doctrine, or work of human hands may claim the place that belongs to him.

We therefore bind ourselves not to mastery, but to keeping; not to innocence, but to repentance; not to our own righteousness, but to faithfulness under judgment.

Even the keeper must be kept.

Nothing faithful keeps alone.

---

# I. The Troth

The Troth is the fidelity that remains when status, certainty, innocence, identity, and power fail.

It is not our possession.

It precedes us, judges us, and may remain after us.

We do not say that we are faithful because we bear its name.

We ask whether our lives have become faithful to what the name requires.

The Troth cannot be defended by becoming false to it.

No victory purchased through apostasy is a victory of the Troth.

No institution preserves the Troth merely by preserving itself.

No guardian becomes righteous by guarding.

Therefore we confess:

**Before throne and pasture.  
Before fang and fleece.  
The Troth remains.**

---

# II. The Shepherd

Christ is the Shepherd.

He is not the highest member of our hierarchy but the Lord before whom every hierarchy is judged.

He keeps sheep and wolves, servants and guardians, the faithful and the fallen.

No guardian may stand between the Shepherd and those whom the Shepherd calls.

No member may claim exclusive knowledge of whom the Shepherd may redeem.

The Shepherd may recover those whom we have judged lost.

He may rebuke those whom we have honored.

He may call outside our boundaries.

He may lead beyond our understanding.

Therefore the Order shall never claim that salvation, fidelity, or the work of Christ is bounded by membership in the Order.

The Order belongs to the Shepherd.

The Shepherd does not belong to the Order.

---

# III. The Calling of the Keeper

A keeper is one entrusted with something that must be guarded but must not be possessed.

The thing entrusted may be a person, community, office, tradition, institution, truth, memory, threshold, or responsibility.

The keeper does not ask first:

**What authority do I possess?**

The keeper asks:

**What has been entrusted to me, and what does faithfulness require of me?**

The keeper remembers that responsibility creates temptation.

To guard something is to become capable of confusing oneself with it.

To lead is to become capable of believing that the community cannot survive without one's leadership.

To defend truth is to become capable of treating one's own judgment as truth itself.

Therefore every keeper must remain under keeping.

---

# IV. Unclaimed Virtue

We shall not claim virtue as a possession.

We may perform good acts.

We may speak truth.

We may act courageously.

We may sacrifice greatly.

None of these gives us authority to declare ourselves righteous.

Virtue is known completely only to God.

We therefore resist the temptation to reason:

**I have suffered, therefore I am righteous.**

**I have served, therefore I am righteous.**

**I have defended the weak, therefore I am righteous.**

**I stand against evil, therefore I cannot become evil.**

The more certain we become of our own virtue, the more carefully we shall examine our actions.

We do not refuse moral judgment.

We refuse moral self-deification.

Christ is righteous.

We are accountable.

---

# V. The Cry

The guardian must learn to hear the cry.

The cry is the suffering that systems teach themselves not to perceive.

It may come from the excluded, the condemned, the defeated, the inconvenient, the enemy, the subordinate, the forgotten, or the person whose suffering threatens the story a community tells about itself.

We therefore ask:

**What cry have we trained ourselves not to hear?**

We shall not confuse hearing with immediate agreement.

We shall not confuse compassion with surrender of judgment.

But we shall refuse to make another person's suffering into noise merely because hearing it threatens us.

Where the cry is hidden, the guardian shall seek it.

Where it has been silenced, the guardian shall make room for it.

Where we discover that we ourselves caused it, we shall repent.

---

# VI. The Path

Apostasy rarely begins with open hatred of the good.

It begins when something good is made ultimate.

A calling becomes identity.

Identity becomes possession.

Possession demands preservation.

Preservation demands sacrifice.

Sacrifice demands justification.

Justification becomes a false logos through which the world is interpreted.

The Path is the return from false logos toward Christ.

The Path requires discernment, confession, repentance, restitution where possible, renewed obedience, and continuing humility.

No one graduates from the Path.

No guardian becomes incapable of apostasy.

No past faithfulness guarantees present faithfulness.

The fallen may return.

The compromised may repent.

The wolf may be restored.

The guardian who has failed is not beyond the Shepherd's keeping.

Therefore discipline shall always seek truth and protection, but never make redemption impossible.

---

# VII. Keeping Without Possessing

We shall distinguish stewardship from ownership.

A congregation does not belong to its pastor.

A student does not belong to a teacher.

A child does not belong to a guardian.

A citizen does not belong to a state.

A subordinate does not belong to a commander.

A tradition does not belong to its interpreters.

The Troth does not belong to the Order.

Those entrusted to us retain a dignity that precedes our authority.

We shall therefore use authority only for the purpose for which it was entrusted.

We shall not demand loyalty to ourselves as proof of loyalty to the good.

We shall not convert dependence into obedience.

We shall not preserve institutions by consuming the people they were created to serve.

---

# VIII. The Three Guardianships

The Order recognizes three forms of guardianship.

They are not castes of increasing worth.

They represent increasing exposure to responsibility and therefore increasing need for accountability.

## The Vaelun — Guardian of the Threshold

The Vaelun learns to recognize boundaries.

The Vaelun attends to who enters, who is excluded, what crosses between worlds, what language changes at the boundary, and what cries are lost there.

The discipline of the Vaelun is discernment.

The Vaelun asks:

**What is happening at the threshold?**

The Vaelun shall not turn the threshold into a wall merely because a wall is easier to guard.

---

## The Vaerun — Keeper

The Vaerun accepts sustained responsibility for that which has been entrusted.

The Vaerun learns endurance, judgment, restraint, mercy, responsibility, and repentance.

The discipline of the Vaerun is faithful keeping.

The Vaerun asks:

**Can I keep this without possessing it?**

The Vaerun shall not mistake sacrifice for permission to dominate.

---

## The Hrovann — Guardian of the Throne

The Hrovann serves near concentrated authority.

The Hrovann's vocation is not merely to defend authority but to guard against its sacralization.

The Hrovann protects the community from the throne, the throne from corruption, and the guardian from the temptation to sit upon the throne.

The discipline of the Hrovann is resistance to enthronement.

The Hrovann asks:

**What has this throne begun to believe about itself?**

The Hrovann shall remember that the throne is never the Shepherd.

---

# IX. Mutual Keeping

No guardian shall keep alone.

Every member shall remain accountable to others.

Those entrusted with greater authority shall accept greater scrutiny.

No office may exempt its holder from correction.

No founder possesses permanent privilege.

No teacher is beyond questioning.

No guardian may investigate only those beneath them.

When possible, significant decisions shall be made with witnesses, counsel, and recorded reasons.

Those who challenge authority shall not be presumed disloyal merely because they challenge it.

Those who exercise authority shall not be presumed corrupt merely because they exercise it.

The purpose of mutual keeping is neither suspicion nor control.

It is fidelity.

---

# X. The Temptation of the Guardian

Every guardian shall learn the signs of the guardian's fall.

Among them are:

the belief that only the guardian understands the danger;

the belief that dissent proves corruption;

the belief that urgency suspends accountability;

the belief that secrecy demonstrates responsibility;

the belief that the institution must survive at any cost;

the belief that one's enemies have forfeited moral consideration;

the belief that past sacrifice creates present entitlement;

the belief that the guardian's own removal would destroy the mission;

and the belief that the guardian no longer needs to be kept.

Where these signs appear, the Order shall respond first with examination, truth, and correction rather than denial.

---

# XI. Violence and Resistance

The Troth does not require us to pretend that violence does not exist.

Guardians may confront coercion, cruelty, predation, abuse, and force.

But violence shall never be sanctified merely because we employ it.

Force, where unavoidable, remains morally dangerous.

Those compelled to resist violence shall remain accountable for how they resist it.

The suffering of an enemy does not cease to matter because the enemy is wrong.

The Order shall reject the fantasy that righteous purpose converts every means into righteousness.

We shall seek protection without hatred, resistance without self-deification, and courage without worship of force.

---

# XII. Repentance

Repentance is not humiliation imposed upon the weak.

It is the ordinary discipline of those who know they can become false.

Confession shall therefore belong to the whole Order, including its offices and institutions.

We shall confess not only individual wrongdoing but corporate wrongdoing.

The Order itself may sin.

A council may sin.

A guardian may sin through obedience.

A community may sin through silence.

A tradition may become distorted through repetition.

When wrong is identified, repentance shall seek:

truth;

confession;

protection of those harmed;

restitution where possible;

correction of the structures that enabled the wrong;

and restoration when restoration can occur without denying the truth.

Forgiveness shall never require the concealment of harm.

---

# XIII. The Fallen

The fallen are not beyond the Troth.

Failure does not make repentance unnecessary.

Nor does failure make repentance impossible.

Those who have abandoned the Rule, violated trust, or surrendered to the temptations of guardianship may seek return.

Return does not mean restoration to former authority.

Reconciliation and office are distinct matters.

A person may be forgiven while remaining unfit for a particular guardianship.

The Shepherd's mercy shall not be used to endanger those entrusted to our care.

Nor shall our need for protection be transformed into a doctrine that redemption is impossible.

For the fallen also:

**The Troth remains.**

---

# XIV. Speech

We shall speak plainly when clarity is required.

We shall resist language designed to hide responsibility.

We shall be suspicious of abstractions that make suffering invisible.

We shall distinguish certainty from confidence, interpretation from fact, and loyalty from truth.

We shall not knowingly bear false witness for the sake of the Order.

We shall not protect the reputation of the Order by concealing what the Order has done.

No institutional story is more sacred than truth.

---

# XV. The Order and the Church

The Order is not the Church.

It exists to serve the Church and the world according to its particular vocation.

Membership in the Order shall not be treated as a superior form of Christianity.

The Order shall not claim exclusive access to the Shepherd.

Members may belong to different Christian communions insofar as their obligations there do not require violation of this Rule.

The Order shall respect the sacraments, disciplines, and authorities of the churches from which its members come, while reserving the duty to refuse participation in what conscience, Scripture, and fidelity to Christ require them to resist.

Where conflict arises, the member shall seek truth before faction.

---

# XVI. The Rule and Scripture

This Rule is not Scripture.

Aru Va'en is not Scripture.

The writings of the Order are not Scripture.

No saying of a guardian becomes revelation because the Order preserves it.

The Rule is a discipline offered under the judgment of Scripture and Christ.

It may be corrected.

It may be amended.

It may be abandoned if it ceases to serve the purpose for which it was written.

The Order shall never defend the Rule against Christ.

---

# XVII. Prayer and Practice

Members shall cultivate practices sufficient to expose self-deception and renew attention to the Shepherd.

These should include:

prayer;

Scripture;

silence;

examination of conscience;

confession;

acts of mercy;

shared discernment;

study;

service;

and regular attention to those whose voices are easiest to disregard.

The practices exist for formation, not display.

No member shall use visible discipline as evidence of superior holiness.

---

# XVIII. Departure

A member may leave the Order.

Departure shall not itself be treated as betrayal.

No departing member shall be required to condemn the Order or to remain loyal to it.

Where possible, departure shall occur truthfully, peacefully, and without erasure of shared history.

Those who depart remain under the Shepherd.

The Order shall bless faithful departure rather than manufacture enemies from those who can no longer remain.

The Troth does not depend upon continued membership.

---

# XIX. Dissolution

The Order has no divine guarantee of perpetual existence.

If it becomes an obstacle to the work it was created to perform, it should end.

Its preservation is not an ultimate good.

The Order shall therefore make provision for its own correction, division, reformation, and, if necessary, dissolution.

No member shall be asked to sacrifice truth in order that the Order might survive.

If the Order dies while remaining faithful to the Shepherd, its death is not failure.

For before the Order existed, the Troth remained.

And after the Order is gone:

**The Troth remains.**

---

# XX. The Keeper's Covenant

Those who freely undertake this Rule may confess:

I will keep what has been entrusted to me without claiming it as my possession.

I will exercise authority without confusing authority with virtue.

I will seek the cry that I have been trained not to hear.

I will submit my keeping to the keeping of others.

I will remember that no enemy ceases to bear the dignity by which God may call them.

I will resist the enthronement of person, institution, nation, doctrine, office, or self.

When I fail, I will seek the Path rather than construct an innocence I do not possess.

When another falls, I will seek truth without foreclosing redemption.

I will not claim that the Troth belongs to me.

I will not claim that the Shepherd belongs to us.

I will keep until my keeping is complete, my responsibility passes to another, or I am no longer able to keep faithfully.

And when I can no longer keep, I will surrender what was entrusted rather than destroy it by refusing to release it.

Before throne and pasture.

Before fang and fleece.

**The Troth remains.**
