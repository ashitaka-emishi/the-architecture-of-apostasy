# Biblical Intelligence Method for the Troth Project

## Aim

The BI layer tests, constrains, and enriches the constructive Troth theology. It does not merely search Scripture for phrases that sound similar.

## Claim classes

### B1 — Textual / lexical
What does this passage say in its literary and linguistic context?

### B2 — Historical-critical
What can responsibly be said about authorial, social, political, or religious context?

### B3 — Canonical-theological
How does the passage participate in a larger Christian canonical pattern?

### B4 — Constructive theological
How may the Troth framework synthesize B1–B3 for a contemporary theological problem?

### B5 — Mythopoetic
How may *Aru Va'en* symbolize that constructive claim?

### B6 — Liturgical / pastoral
How might a community pray, confess, preach, or practice the claim?

## Rule against certainty borrowing

A B4 constructive category is not made B1 merely by attaching a verse to it. A B5 mythic image is not proof of B3 canonical theology. A B6 liturgical formula is not a lexical definition.

## Passage dossier questions

For every major passage:
1. What problem is the text itself addressing?
2. What are the key terms and disputed translations?
3. What is the immediate literary argument?
4. What rival scholarly readings matter?
5. What claim can be made with high textual confidence?
6. What canonical synthesis is legitimate but exceeds the immediate text?
7. What Troth claim is constructive rather than exegetical?
8. What would falsify or constrain that constructive use?
9. What pastoral misuse should be explicitly prevented?

## Citation policy

For external scholarship, record exact bibliographic data and page numbers before drafting. Never invent quotations or page references. Preserve disconfirming evidence.
