# John 10:1–18 Dossier

## Project use

Primary biblical control for the Troth's shepherd/pasture/fang grammar and for authority as self-giving rather than sacrificial extraction.

## Established homiletical synthesis

The recovered sermon is preserved at:

`source/sermons/The Troth Remains - John 10.md`

Its major constructive claims include:
- the sheep are first taught to hear, not to self-certify as good;
- the pasture may shelter without becoming ultimate;
- the throne is judged by the Shepherd who gives himself rather than consumes the flock;
- the wolf is real, but the wolf must not determine the believer's identity;
- victimhood does not make fleece the measure;
- Christ knows persons by name rather than absorbing them into the collective;
- the hired hand exposes faithfulness when remaining becomes costly;
- return is possible because the Shepherd remains.

## Research cautions

These are constructive/homiletical uses. Future dossier work should distinguish:
- historical/literary questions around John 9–10;
- the sheepfold/gate/shepherd metaphors;
- “thieves and robbers” in context;
- Johannine Christology;
- the relation between voluntary self-giving and later institutional ethics.
