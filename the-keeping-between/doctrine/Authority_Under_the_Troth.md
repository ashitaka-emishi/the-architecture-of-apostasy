# Authority Under the Troth

**Status:** working synthesis.

## Core claim

> **Authority is entrusted, not possessed.**

Authority does not arise from the leader's claim to virtue. It arises when a responsibility to decide, teach, guard, witness, coordinate, or serve is entrusted to someone under a truth that remains above both ruler and ruled.

## Possible grounds of legitimate authority

Authority may arise from:
- office;
- competence;
- experience;
- calling/discernment;
- witness;
- relationship and voluntarily entrusted care;
- necessity in a concrete situation.

None of these entails moral infallibility.

## Decisional authority versus righteousness

A central distinction:

> **Authority determines who must decide. It does not determine who is right.**

A Vaerun, Hrovan, presider, elder, commander, teacher, or parent can hold real responsibility while remaining correctable.

## Accountability

The Troth reverses status logic:

> **Greater authority → greater responsibility → greater accountability.**

Rank is not accumulated moral worth. It is accumulated answerability.

## Fruit test

Over time, ask whether an authority:
- requires concealment or permits truth;
- can receive correction;
- bears burdens or exports them downward;
- protects persons or sacrifices them to preserve the institution;
- restrains its own fang;
- hears inconvenient cries;
- permits repentance;
- can say “I was wrong” without collapsing into self-defense;
- remains willing to surrender office when office and vocation separate.

## Apostasy of authority

Authority becomes apostate when the entrusted mediator identifies itself with the Good it was meant to serve:

**responsibility → office → self-preservation → identification of office with good → criticism as betrayal → persons sacrificed to preserve the office/pasture**

The apostolic greeting functions as a constitutional interruption:

**Before throne and pasture.**  
**Before fang and fleece.**  
**The Troth remains!**

The throne is real, but not ultimate.

## Vaerun

Vaerun's authority is best grounded in witness, crossing, experience, responsibility, and service. A recurring literary safeguard should be that another companion can correct him and that he can preserve the correction in his own letters.
