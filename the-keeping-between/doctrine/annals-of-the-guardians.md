# Annals of the Guardians

Within the tradition of **The Troth**, guardianship is not a single office but a threefold vocation: the guarding of the boundary, the crossing of the boundary, and the bearing of the center. These are expressed in the offices of the **Vaelun, Vaerun, and Hrovan**. None possesses authority in itself. Each serves the fidelity disclosed and judged in **The Shepherd**, which Troth language names without possessing. Each remains subject to his judgment and keeping.

## The Vaelun: Guardian of the Threshold

The Vaelun stands at the boundary between the ordered world and what lies beyond it. The Vaelun watches, discerns, warns, and protects. Its question is not merely whether something is dangerous, but whether a crossing is properly ordered.

This is a foundational form of guardianship because every community requires boundaries. Without distinction, there can be no form; without discernment, there can be no faithful hospitality.

Yet the Vaelun faces a characteristic temptation: to mistake the boundary for the thing being protected. A gate exists for passage as well as defense. When guarding becomes an end in itself, vigilance becomes exclusion and fidelity hardens into fear.

The Shepherd therefore stands before the threshold as its measure. The Vaelun does not decide what is good merely because it stands at the gate. Its discernment and its use of Troth language remain answerable to him.

## The Vaerun: Guardian Who Crosses

The Vaerun stands at the veil but receives a calling distinct from threshold-keeping. The Vaerun is one who must sometimes **cross it**.

The Vaerun represents the paradox at the heart of faithful guardianship: sometimes one must leave the place one has sworn to protect in order to remain faithful to what that place was meant to serve.

The Vaerun therefore guards not simply by resisting movement but by discerning when movement itself has become necessary.

If the Vaelun asks,

**“What may cross?”**

the Vaerun asks,

**“What must I cross in order to remain faithful?”**

The Vaerun is thus a safeguard against institutional captivity. A guardian who cannot leave the gate may eventually become unable to distinguish the gate from the Good.

The Shepherd frees the Vaerun to cross because his fidelity is not contained within any particular boundary.

## The Hrovan: Guardian of the Center

The Hrovan bears the hall.

At the center, the Hrovan is concerned with continuity, cohesion, memory, order, and the life of the gathered people. In archetypal terms, the Hrovan approaches the role of the **throne guardian**.

But the Hrovan does not possess an unconditional obligation to the throne.

This distinction is essential.

The Hrovan keeps the center only insofar as the center remains ordered toward the fidelity disclosed in the Shepherd. A throne that betrays that fidelity has forfeited the very authority the Hrovan was called to defend.

The fallen Hrovan says:

*The throne must remain because I have sworn to keep it.*

The faithful Hrovan says:

*I keep because the Shepherd has entrusted keeping. Therefore even the throne remains subject to his judgment.*

The Hrovan thus bears continuity without making continuity divine.

## The Three Guardianships

Together the three offices form a coordinated architecture of keeping. They are different callings, not successive stages, training grades, or ranks of advancement.

**The Vaelun guards the boundary.**

**The Vaerun crosses the boundary.**

**The Hrovan bears the center.**

None is sufficient alone.

A world composed only of Vaelun would become walls.

A world composed only of Vaerun would become endless crossing without habitation.

A world composed only of Hrovan would become permanence without correction.

Faithful order requires all three: discernment, transformation, and continuity.

Their unity lies not in the similarity of their tasks but in the object of their fidelity.

They serve the Shepherd's purposes through creaturely fidelity.

## The Troth Before Every Created Role

The apostolic greeting expresses the hierarchy directly:

**Before throne and pasture.**  
**Before fang and fleece.**  
**The Troth remains.**

The throne represents authority and ordered power.

The pasture represents the community and its habitation.

The fang represents the guardian and the strength by which it keeps.

The fleece represents those understood to be protected.

The greeting places all four beneath a fidelity none can possess, while the myth's close names the Shepherd as personally prior to that confession.

The Troth does not belong to the throne.

The Troth does not belong to the guardian.

The Troth does not belong even to the flock.

Rather, the Shepherd judges throne, guardian, and flock, including whether their claimed fidelity truly participates in his.

This prevents guardianship from becoming self-righteous. The guardian cannot point to its sacrifice, vigilance, courage, or endurance as proof that it is good. Keeping does not justify the keeper.

Faithfulness remains subject to judgment.

## The Shepherd

Above the three guardianships stands neither a greater guardian nor a more powerful throne, but **The Shepherd**.

The Shepherd represents the one who knows what must ultimately be kept.

The guardian sees from a position.

The Shepherd sees the flock.

The guardian receives an office.

The Shepherd knows the purpose of the office.

The guardian keeps something entrusted to it.

The Shepherd judges whether that keeping remains faithful.

Therefore even the most faithful guardian cannot become its own final authority.

This truth is expressed in the saying:

**Even the keeper must be kept.  
Nothing faithful keeps alone.**

The Shepherd does not abolish guardianship. He redeems it from isolation.

The Vaelun can open the gate because the gate is not God.

The Vaerun can cross the veil because the place left behind is not God.

The Hrovan can relinquish the throne because the throne is not God.

And the fallen keeper can return because failure has not placed it beyond the Shepherd's keeping.

## The Meaning of Guardianship

The three guardianships therefore describe more than offices. They describe three necessary forms of faithful responsibility.

There are things that must be protected.

There are boundaries that must be crossed.

There are communities that must be borne.

Wisdom lies in knowing which form of keeping a moment requires.

The confession that the Troth remains prevents the guardian from identifying its task with the Good.

The Shepherd judges that confession and prevents the guardian from imagining that it keeps alone.

And so the oldest truth of the guardians remains the simplest:

The boundary is not eternal.

The throne is not eternal.

The guardian is not eternal.

**The Troth remains.**
