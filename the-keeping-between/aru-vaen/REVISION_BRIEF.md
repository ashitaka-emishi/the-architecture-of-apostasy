# Aru Va'en — Active Revision Brief

## Purpose

Revise the myth at the level of **symbolic, theological, ecclesial, Christological, and narrative structure**, not merely prose style.

## Source baseline problem

The frozen PDF and V3 draft contain extensive physical combat, wounding, killing, and endurance-through-violence. Those passages remain useful evidence of the earlier architecture but are not automatically the intended final resolution.

## Current mandate

### Opening control

Preserve the definitive PDF invocation exactly:

> Before throne and pasture, before fang and fleece, there was the Troth.
>
> Before the Troth had a name, it had an ache.

Treat the ache as old mythic personification for creaturely apprehension of torn relation, not an autonomous force, law, or being prior to Christ. Do not explain that metaphysics in the opening. Let the immediate cries ground the ache in the world's torn places, and let the close establish the final order: the Shepherd before the Troth, the cry before the creaturely vow, and love before grief.

Use the accepted governing formulation: **The Troth names the fidelity of God disclosed and enacted in Jesus Christ, and the creaturely calling to participate in that fidelity by the Spirit.** This is constructive theology rather than biblical lexicography. In the myth, carry it through Christological image, relation, action, and the Shepherd-prior close rather than explanatory doctrinal prose.

Distinguish persons by present action and condition rather than fixed moral species. Use `assailant` for one presently attacking, `afflicted` for one suffering, and `fallen` for an accountable but still return-capable agent. Treat Black Sun manifestations as unstable near-likenesses rather than a disposable race, prefer concrete verbs to taxonomic labels, and let the language change when action or condition changes.

### 1. Keep violence real; remove violence as the solution

The myth must not pretend physical and spiritual violence do not exist. The Host/fallen assault can remain dangerous and intense. The central faithful action, however, should shift toward **non-cooperation, bearing, shielding, naming, refusing deformation, truth-telling, and peaceful resistance** rather than reciprocal killing.

The wolves follow a strict least-harm refrain:

> Do not hit when holding is enough.
>
> Do not maim when hitting is enough.

They may hold, shield, carry, interpose, open escape, and, when holding fails, throw immediate attackers back with controlled bodily force. Fang and claw must not draw an attacker's blood. When a wolf falters in restraint, the Shepherd's grief becomes visibly greater.

Under D-081, this refrain governs the revision but is not quoted or explained inside Eternal Dawn. The candidate should make it visible through a few costly actions beneath the Shepherd's call and judgment.

The circle is permeable: it opens inward for the wounded and returning while closing against immediate assault. Wolves brace with shoulder, flank, and body; two may pin without tearing; jaws may carry or hold fur without puncture. A controlled throw restores space and ends at the boundary. It is never followed by pursuit, punishment, or seizure of ground. Wounded wolves rotate inward.

Their outward path-making, interposition, carrying, and bounded opening mirror the Shepherd's inward gathering, separating, tending, receiving, and reordering of members caught in the four curses. **The Shepherd makes room within bodies; the wolves make room between bodies.** Vaerun falters first: after throwing an attacker back, he begins to pursue, another wolf holds him at the boundary, the Shepherd's grief visibly grows, and Vaerun returns to his place. The wolves still bleed, tire, and lose ground.

### 2. Preserve the psychological/spiritual assault

The Black Sun should still expose the four curses and their embodied marks:
- the fang turned inward, deforming power through pressure against one's own throat and the taste or smell of iron before a wound;
- the eye sealed shut, deforming perception through a dry film like ash or wax and false smoothness where the world has depth;
- the paw washed clean of blood, deforming responsibility through unnatural smoothness, scentlessness, and false innocence;
- the mouth closed around the cry, deforming witness through stale breath and a trapped vibration behind the teeth;
- accusation using beloved voices and near-likeness;
- pressure to become what one fears.

Let the Black Sun work through true fragments of wound, failure, fear, betrayal, and misused authority. Its deformation is the false total verdict that isolates those truths from relation, proportion, mercy, correction, and return. The Shepherd neither dismisses what is true nor accepts condemnation as final identity; he separates exposure from the lie made from it and reopens responsible action.

Call these manifestations **the marks of the curses**. Their texture and scent deform what companions know of one another; they do not supply an infallible odor of guilt. The marks can pass over faithful and fallen alike and remain distinct from the marks of Vethra and the provisional institutional Sigil. Little Vey's smelling of remembrance is a particular relational perception, not a general curse-detecting power.

The Shepherd answers these deformations by sorting within bodies rather than between persons. He separates without severing: the inward fang is drawn away from the throat and returned toward protection; the sealed eye is opened to depth, truth, and relation; the falsely clean paw regains creaturely texture, scent, weight, and responsibility without requiring a new bloodstain; and the mouth closed around the cry is loosened so cry and truthful answer may pass. No member is cut away or discarded. The Shepherd reorders each member within the whole body and the whole body beneath himself. Do not confuse an imposed curse-mark with a wound, disability, bodily difference, or incomplete healing.

Retain the White Moon as reflected ordinary mercy: received tenderness, restraint, recognition, and care that persist at the edge of the Black Sun's exposure. It neither saves nor opposes the Black Sun as an equal power, and it does not make the Shepherd's work possible. It is not extinguished; its pale light is gathered into true morning. **The White Moon does not make morning. It remembers the light by which morning may be known.**

The ordeal must remain costly even if the faithful no longer win by tearing bodies apart.

### 3. Open redemption to the fallen

The fallen must remain persons capable of return. In the myth's constructive grammar, **salvation is allowing oneself to participate in the proper ordering of the Shepherd**. His prior call, judgment, mercy, and opened room make the participation possible; yielded consent is not self-salvation. The proper ordering belongs to the Shepherd, not to the wolves, circle, Vaerun, office, or institution.

The first returning sheep hears and turns, stops participating in the assault, refuses the next impulse or command to harm, and opens an escape for a smaller or injured creature. The circle opens; the smaller creature passes first, and the returning sheep follows without any fang closing upon either. The sheep bears the sealed-eye deformation. Once it crosses, the Shepherd lays his thumb against the ash- or wax-like film and draws away what the Black Sun laid there, opening rather than removing the eye. The sheep first sees the wolves holding the passage without biting and recognizes them as a sign that it is returning to its correctly ordered position beneath the Shepherd; it then looks to the Shepherd for whom they hold the passage. The wolves point beyond themselves through restrained, properly related keeping; they do not cause, certify, or measure salvation. The Host and flock now see that cessation and return have somewhere to go, though some return, some withdraw, and some continue the assault. Reception does not declare innocence, restore trust, or make the sheep a guardian. Confession begins in changed action; spoken confession, restitution, repaired trust, and any later responsibility remain unfinished.

Under D-082, this is the only ash-covered eye opened in Eternal Dawn. Let the sealed-eye mark first appear on the same sheep far below while the Shepherd reorders fang, paw, and voice within the circle; its later opened eye completes the fourfold sequence. In the remembered prose, call the persons bearing beloved but wrong voices **the fallen**, following the definitive PDF. Reserve `near-likeness` for analysis of their unstable forms rather than using it as a narrative people-name.

The governing distinction:

> Failure is real. The fallen are not the measure. The Troth remains.

### 4. Rework the circle

The circle should become less a battle formation and more a **communion of keeping**: bodies and voices refusing to surrender another to isolation, accusation, dehumanization, or forced identity.

### 5. Rework the Aruvan

The Aruvan cannot become the final sacred body. The Shepherd keeps the Aruvan.

Use only `Aruvan`. `Aruvann` was a misspelling in active development material, not a distinct term, being, office, or commentary category.

Retain **Aruvan and the Living Lamb as two faces of one creaturely communion beneath the Shepherd**. Aruvan shows that communion from the side of accumulated keeping; the Living Lamb shows the same communion from the side of life kept, restored, gathered, and becoming active companion. They are not spouses, independent beings, or complementary saving powers. The individual lamb remains itself and becomes the first visible participant in the larger Living Lamb without metamorphosis or deification.

After healing, the lamb leaves Vaerun's temporary shelter, waits beside the returning sheep, and walks with it toward the Shepherd; others may join. It gathers by accompaniment rather than command, absorption, possession, or demanded sacrifice. In the later vision, Aruvan lowers itself and opens while the Living Lamb moves through that opening toward the Shepherd with the gathered and returning. Their movements need not match.

Aruvan is redeemed through **relinquishment without annihilation**. Its closed, self-contained body loosens rather than surviving intact or being destroyed. What had gathered into stature returns to the living as accountable, distributed memory, responsibility, practice, and distinct callings beneath the Shepherd. Breath and warmth may remain as vision-language, but not as an operative ancestor, autonomous spirit, or invisible institution. This does not prescribe dissolution for every institution; it denies any accumulated form the right to make its own survival the meaning of fidelity.

> **Even the keeper must be kept. Nothing faithful keeps alone.**

This should control the relation of office, ancestry, communion, and Christ.

### 6. Rework Black Sun → morning

Morning must arise neither from defeating an enemy nor from forgetting the ordeal. The desired transition is:

**exposure → truthful recognition → non-cooperation with false identity → bearing/keeping → repentance/return → re-gathering under the Shepherd → morning**

Morning is an ordinary earthly dawn on the same wounded hill and a genuine firstfruits image of new creation in Christ:

> **Morning is not the world to come. It is this wounded world beginning to bear its promised shape beneath the Shepherd.**

Keep the hill, blood, dead, wounds, grief, accountability, and unfinished repair. Let honest light, distinct scents, moving water, living grass, recognizable creaturely identity, healing, relinquished possession, active companionship, and accountable return mirror the promised creation without becoming heaven or literal resurrection. Harrow remains dead; his absence points beyond Morning toward resurrection still to come. No ancestor returns, wound shines as holiness, or suffering produces the dawn.

Give that foretaste bodily form in a shared meal:

> **Morning is not the Feast. It is bread eaten in its promise.**

Count the missing, tend the wounded, and cover the dead first. Then let the Hrovan and the Truvane arrive ordinarily with food and water rather than regalia. The Shepherd receives the provision, gives thanks, and makes room for the wounded, guardians, flock, and those beginning accountable return to eat. The meal culminates the proper ordering of Hrovan, Vaelun, Vaerun, Gravane, Truvane, Lamb and flock, Vethra, and Troth beneath him. This is relational order, not ranked seating: the Hrovan brings provision and confession rather than presiding; each guardian office serves according to its distinct calling without becoming a ladder; the Lamb and flock participate actively; and those beginning accountable return may eat without being declared innocent or restored to office. Vethra is borne subtly in flesh rather than seated or displayed, while the Troth remains as fidelity enacted and named in these relations rather than a guest, food, vessel, or power beside the Shepherd. Shared hunger places fang and fleece together beneath the Shepherd, but feeding does not restore trust, complete restitution, or finish reconciliation. Harrow's absence remains perceptible. Kill no creature, especially no lamb, to provide the meal. Permit Eucharistic and eschatological resonance without instituting a sacrament, repeating the words of institution, or making the food salvific. Keep living Vey beside Vaerun so the close arises from embodied fellowship.

Stage the meal as an open ring around the Shepherd with several passages and no head table. Harrow's absence remains an unfilled space without food for the dead. After the Hrovan's confession and the gathering's silence, the Shepherd receives the provision, gives thanks without quoted words of institution, breaks the bread, and makes it common. The Hrovan pours water for the wounded before himself; Gravane attend to truthful memory and actual need; Truvane distribute bread and water, including the candidate who declined; Vaelun keep the passages open and rotate into the meal; and Vaerun carries food across the former boundary to flock and returning persons before coming back among his companions. The wounded lamb walks beside the returning sheep toward the Shepherd, and the flock approaches freely. Let Vethra be perceived in relation rather than certified, and let the Troth remain through the ordering rather than an object.

As the meal quiets, the lamb rests against the Shepherd and his scarred human hands remain visible. Living Vey sits beside and quietly steadies Vaerun, then says exactly, **"Before the Troth, there was the Shepherd."** Vaerun completes the remembered close in narration: **"Before the vow was the cry. Before grief was love. We do not keep alone."** Vey names the Shepherd's priority rather than unmasking him.

### 7. Use the Greeting of Departure when the called leave

The Apostolic Greeting remains established for its other uses. In the myth's departure scene, use the distinct Greeting of Departure:

**One:** Though the road divide us.

**Other:** Though the night conceal us.

**Both:** **The Troth remains!**

The source's older “there was the Troth” remains important as narrative theology, but the communal greeting uses “The Troth remains.”

Hrovan and the gathered Truvane say **"Though the road divide us"**; Vaerun and the twelve named Vaelun called to cross answer **"Though the night conceal us"**; and the whole hall says **"The Troth remains!"** The collective voices preserve the settled structure while joining those who remain and those who leave in the final confession. Do not add the Apostolic Greeting beside it.

Before the commission and calling, the Hrovan says publicly, **"The cry binds us to answer. It does not tell us what answer will keep faith."** One of the gathered Gravane objects, **"The cry has told us whom we may not abandon. It has not told us whom to strike."** The objection must alter the commission toward witness, protection, and discernment. The Hrovan's admitted limit does not release him from responsibility, and the objection does not make its speaker infallible.

When the Hrovan arrives ordinarily with the Truvane and provision before the Morning meal, he confesses publicly and exactly: **"I sent you beneath a charge I had not understood. The stone seat entrusted me to answer. It did not make my answer faithful."** This owns his decision, preserves entrusted responsibility, and denies that office certifies righteousness. Add no fourth line. Do not make the confession a renunciation of the Hrovan calling, a claim that answering the cry was itself wrong, an appeal to the wounds or dead for justification, or a new credential of humility. The gathering answers with silence rather than absolution or acclaim; the Hrovan remains beside the provision awaiting the Shepherd's ordering.

### 8. Treat the PDF as definitive over V3

The frozen PDF is the controlling myth source. The recovered V3 draft remains useful evidence of alternate development, but no V3 wording, structure, continuity, or theology may override the PDF without an explicit recorded revision.

### 9. Keep Little Vey alive

Little Vey survives the Keeping as a living, embodied companion and later writes to Vaerun. The revised ending must make his survival clear. His only lasting wound is one torn ear, received after he hears the sheep's soft answer, which leaves him hearing poorly on that side. Remove the damaged eye, limp, amputation, and accumulated heroic mutilation. His post-Keeping correspondence gives him continuing agency rather than reducing him to a memory, symbol, source of ancestral power, or martyr-hero.

Harrow is the sole named companion who dies during the Keeping. He is carried within the circle and tended before dying from wounds received in the assault. **Harrow does not die because he holds; he dies despite being held.** His death purchases nothing, does not strengthen the circle, and does not certify him. He remains absent from the living company in earthly morning, while his dead flesh bears Vethra without animation, preservation, radiance, or resurrection.

Murn survives aged and wounded, without the restoration of his young face; his humor remains living companionship. Sable, Brindle, Keld, Ash, Rook, Yarrow, Thorn, Ostra, and Fen also survive. Keep the accepted wound roster restrained: Vaerun has a serious side scar; Murn has damaged ribs and permanently roughened breath; Sable's weakened voice sometimes breaks in song; Vey has only his settled torn ear; and Brindle's back remains bent and painful. Keld, Ash, Rook, Yarrow, Thorn, Ostra, and Fen receive no newly specified lasting injury from the Keeping. Ostra's pre-existing rib scar and the deaths of her three brothers remain part of her history. Mention the injuries briefly and never turn one into a credential, acquired gift, or emblem of office.

Every companion who passed through the Keeping bears Vethra, including Harrow's dead flesh. Vethra is a subtle mark but not a scar: it is testimony of the Troth upon flesh through which it has passed, understood beneath the Shepherd as testimony to received participation rather than an autonomous incarnation of the Troth. In the symbolic grammar shared with `Vaelun` as coal-under-ash and `Vey` as ember or last light under ash, Vethra is hidden first light of the fuller light to come at the eschaton. It may appear in the living through posture, voice, movement, attention, scent, touch, or relation. Gravane and Truvane perceive it chiefly but fallibly. Its bearers seem almost `off` in the opposite way from the fallen: the fallen near-likeness carries missing or imitated relation, while Vethra makes familiar flesh subtly strange because received relation remains present.

Vethra testifies to costly fidelity considered and borne through remaining, restraint, relinquishment, grief, correction, responsibility, and vulnerability. Cost may leave a wound, but Vethra is not the wound, payment for light, a reward for suffering, or proof that enough was sacrificed. Harrow's dead flesh bears it without waking, shining, preservation, operative presence, or early resurrection. Establish Vethra across the whole company through the accepted communal frame, then pause only over Harrow and Ostra. Murn and Keld remain bearers without separate Morning portraits because tending and communal action already carry their relations. Vaerun does not certify his own Vethra, while Vey displays his through embodied presence beside Vaerun. Never make Vethra a credential, curse-mark, institutional Sigil, supernatural guilt detector, or independent light beside Christ.

### 10. Keep the myth central and the Shepherd at its center

Institutional drafts remain downstream applications that may never be enacted. They cannot become the interpretive center of the myth. The Shepherd remains the Christological center who keeps, judges, and gathers every guardian, office, circle, and possible institution.

The Shepherd has no staff. That detail appears only in non-controlling V3 and must not be imported from it. His keeping is shown through embodied presence, scarred hands, voice, movement, judgment, protection, gathering, and the room he makes.

Do not add an opening Scriptural superscription that identifies him before the narration begins. His identity is neither hidden nor treated as a twist. Make him recognizably Christological from his first appearance through image and action. **Vey's ending names his priority rather than unmasking him.**

During the struggle the Shepherd works within the circle, and the wolves' least-harm resistance at the circumference mirrors his prior work rather than enabling it. His continual call and Little Vey's first notice of a lost sheep answering are settled. The sheep hears the Shepherd, begins to remember, and answers with a cry too soft for the other wolves; remembrance changes its scent. Vey hears the cry and smells remembrance but never articulates what he notices during the ordeal; he seeks Vaerun. This alerts Vaerun first, and Vaerun's changed attention then alerts the others. The Shepherd remains the one who calls the lost by name. D-065 settles the central mirror as room made within bodies and between bodies; D-066 settles its public recognition through the first returning sheep's opened eye.

Vey seeks Vaerun out of faithfulness to him and trust in his calling. This is Vey's enacted witness of the Troth, not proof that Vaerun is righteous or the measure. Vey refuses to abandon Vaerun to failure during the ordeal and later refuses to abandon him to greatness: his corrective letter continues the same faithfulness through remembrance and rebuke.

The first visible act of return is almost imperceptible: the lost sheep hears the Shepherd, begins to remember, and moves ever so subtly toward him. The sheep's answering cry is too soft for the other wolves, and remembrance changes its scent. Vey hears the cry and smells remembrance, then seeks Vaerun without explanation. This orientation is not completed repentance, restored trust, or repair. Vey's perception and seeking make the turn perceptible first to Vaerun and then to the others; later consequential acts must grow from this small movement.

Do not overuse Little Vey. His chief action during the ordeal is to function as quiet support that steadies Vaerun: he hears what is nearly lost, smells remembrance, seeks Vaerun, remains faithful to him, and calls him back into relation. Do not make Vey the hidden interpreter, universal sensor, lamb-deliverer, or engine of every turn. His few appearances and words should carry weight, while other companions retain agency and the Shepherd remains the center.

The Shepherd progressively heals the lamb within the circle while assault continues. He supports its breathing, cleans blood and mud from its wounds, binds them with strips from his torn robe, steadies its wounded body with his scarred hands, and continues calling and calming it. Beneath this care, bleeding stops, breathing steadies, torn flesh begins to close, and strength gradually returns beyond what ordinary first aid could accomplish in such conditions. There is no flash, glow, incantation, staff, instant restoration, or narrated mechanism. This is not an isolated cure: bodily restoration gives creaturely form to renewed relation and proper ordering beneath the Shepherd.

During that incomplete healing the lamb begins to remember what it is. The Shepherd's prior call, presence, and care begin and sustain the restoration; the lamb does not earn healing through response. Its ear turns first and then its head turns toward the Shepherd's voice while it remains wounded in his hands. It does not yet stand, speak, bleat an answer, or display sudden strength. Let breath, posture, and later chosen movement grow from this first attention into active companionship with others rather than explanatory speech or self-display. The wounds do not transfer to the Shepherd or purchase healing. Receiving their reality deepens his grief, and that grief strengthens the circle as room, steadiness, renewed capacity to hold, and care for the wounded. It is not the healing mechanism, magical reinforcement, or combat power. The lamb remains creaturely and wounded while genuinely returning; injury, disability, deformity, or incomplete cure must never imply sin, failed faith, lesser worth, or disordered relation. Healing does not identify the lamb with Christ, make its suffering redemptive currency, or reduce salvation to bodily cure. Keep this distinct from the lost sheep beginning to remember the Shepherd who calls it: Vey hears and smells remembrance only in that sheep, not in the lamb.

### 11. Preserve distinct callings; remove the implied ladder

Vaelun, Vaerun, and Hrovan are distinct callings rather than stages of advancement. Use `Hrovan`; `Hrovann` is a misspelling. Vaerun remains the crossing vocation, while Hrovan bears the hall or center. Revise the PDF passages that imply Vaerun is being prepared to become Hrovan; the myth must not foreshadow that as his future.

Before and during the hill, the narrator is **a Vaerun**, one bearer of the crossing vocation. Only afterward, as the Keeping becomes widely remembered, do the many call him **the Vaerun**. That definite title is accrued public stature, not a new office, promotion, or proof of righteousness. Little Vey's letter later addresses the danger created by this change in how others name him.

The narrator's personal name remains unstated in the myth. `Vaelun` identifies his threshold calling and `Vaerun` the crossing office he bears; neither is his personal name. Vey's later remembrance of when only the companions knew his name refers to that undisclosed personal name. In Night, explain Vaelun, Vaerun, and Hrovan sparsely through their related places, responsibilities, charges, trusts, and burdens. Preserve `name` chiefly for the opening invocation and the persons remembered beneath the banners.

After Murn's calling, one frightened unnamed candidate who is not ready for this crossing says exactly, **"Not this crossing."** The Gravane gives the same brow-to-brow gesture used in calling and answers, **"None of us is the measure."** The Gravane does not speak his name for the crossing and moves on, while the candidate remains among the Truvane, continues answering, speaks with those who remain in the Greeting of Departure, and joins the whole hall's final acclamation. The answer neither shames nor compels the candidate. It refuses to make assent, refusal, personal strength, or self-judged insufficiency the measure, and it must not recast the decline as failure, sin, or moral superiority.

Limit individual verbal responses during the naming. The companions' source gestures and brief character beats are sufficient assent, while the Truvane retains the communal **"Aru va'en"** after each name. Preserve Harrow's trembling and brow touch, Murn's age and brief humor, and Little Vey's look toward Vaerun, whispered naming of him, and recognition by the Gravane. Do not flatten or cut those three calling portraits.

### 12. Preserve the old liturgical voice; let the myth keep

Retain the old, liturgical feel of the definitive PDF through oral cadence, ritual repetition, elemental imagery, measured solemnity, and remembered formula. Carry theological clarification through scene, image, recurrence, and voice wherever possible rather than turning the myth into modern exposition. The myth should itself serve as a keeper of the Troth by preserving memory, guarding what is entrusted, exposing possession, and recalling hearers beneath the Shepherd. It does not thereby possess the Troth or acquire Scriptural, revelatory, or Christological authority.

### 13. Require Scriptural and Christological review before final acceptance

Every material revision remains provisional until it has received the thorough Scriptural review specified in the passage change map. The review must distinguish biblical exegesis from constructive, mythopoetic, and liturgical claims; preserve rival readings and inferential steps; and test the Shepherd and every governing symbol against the canonical witness to Christ. Narrative polish or internal coherence is not sufficient for final acceptance.

### 14. Make repetition carry meaning

Reduce the frequency of `old` in the active prose. Keep it where age, inheritance, ritual memory, or long duration needs emphasis, and let concrete images carry the rest. Do not merely exchange it for another repeated synonym.

Reduce the opening's repeated statements of `fear`. Vaerun hesitates because he knows answering the cry and crossing the veil will require costly fidelity. In Night, let him fear that the crossing will find him wanting before the office and the hall's trust; do not let him explain that he thinks he must become the measure. The later revelation supplies that understanding: Vaerun is not the measure; the Shepherd is. Let the opening embody the error through reluctance, trembling, silence, and the burdens represented beneath the banners.

## Later source evidence for the revision

The institutional source package and Little Vey letter ingested on 2026-08-09 are frozen source artifacts, not automatic current decisions. They add the following usable evidence:

- **Little Vey's vocation:** his letter describes smallness as the capacity to see from below, enter places where greater strength cannot stand, hear cries that do not reach high places, and approach wounds that recoil from strength. This should guide the replacement of his old mutilation-and-combat sequence.
- **Resistance and force:** the full Rule says that force, where unavoidable, remains morally dangerous and accountable; righteous purpose does not sanctify every means. D-030 now fixes the choreography at hold first, throw back without drawing blood when holding fails, and never maim or kill.
- **Return:** the Rule joins return to discernment, confession, repentance, restitution where possible, renewed fidelity, and continuing humility. It distinguishes reconciliation from restoration to office.
- **Corporate guardianship:** the Constitution supplies concrete analogies for the Aruvan problem: institutional self-examination, amendment, peaceful separation, relinquishment, and dissolution rather than preservation at any cost.
- **Sigil and Vethra:** the frozen Mark source explicitly distinguishes its voluntary institutional graphic from Vethra, which belongs to the Shepherd. Active vocabulary calls that graphic the Sigil. Vethra is a subtle mark and costly first-light testimony borne upon every companion's flesh, including Harrow in death, not a scar, membership badge, credential, curse-mark, institutional Sigil, possession, or payment.

The institutional conflicts are now controlled by D-022 through D-024: the proposed Order remains unformed and provisional, while active institutional drafts must use the myth's Vaelun, Vaerun, and Hrovan callings without a ladder of advancement.

## Required impact check for every major revision

Before accepting a change, ask:

1. Does it preserve Christ as the final measure?
2. Does it strengthen or weaken Unclaimed Virtue?
3. Does it accidentally make the Host morally self-certifying?
4. Does it make violence unreal, or does it refuse violence-as-identity?
5. Does it leave a credible path of return for the fallen?
6. Does it change the Liturgy of the Troth?
7. Does it change a proposed Letter of Vaerun?
8. Does it alter the meaning of throne, pasture, fang, fleece, cry, or keeping?
9. Does it preserve Little Vey's survival and later correspondence with Vaerun?
10. If it uses a V3 variant, is the departure from the definitive PDF explicit?
11. Does it keep the myth centered on the Shepherd rather than a possible institution?
12. Does it preserve the three offices as distinct callings without foreshadowing Vaerun as a future Hrovan?
13. Does it preserve the old liturgical register and allow the myth to perform keeping without claiming possession or authority over the Troth?
14. Has the material change received documented Scriptural and Christological review, or is it still clearly marked provisional?
15. Does repetition of `old` and `fear` carry real ritual or psychological development, and does Vaerun's mistaken burden yield to the Shepherd as measure?
16. Does physical resistance use the least harmful effective action, avoid drawing attackers' blood, and make the Shepherd's grief visible when restraint fails?
17. Does it preserve Aruvan and the Living Lamb as two faces of one creaturely communion beneath the Shepherd while keeping both distinct from Christ?
18. Is the Shepherd recognizable as Christ from his first appearance without an opening label or a final-act identity reveal?
19. Does the wolves' work at the circumference mirror the Shepherd's work within, with his continual call beginning return and Vey noticing the first answer?
20. Does Morning remain an earthly firstfruits of new creation in Christ without erasing death, wounds, accountability, unfinished repair, or resurrection still to come?
21. Does the Morning meal embody a foretaste of the Feast without becoming a new sacrament, a completed reconciliation, or a meal that conceals Harrow's absence and the work still owed?
