## Governing revision policy

### Preserve the old liturgical register

The revision must retain the old, liturgical feel of the definitive PDF: oral cadence, ritual repetition, elemental imagery, measured solemnity, and language that can bear communal remembrance. Theological clarification should ordinarily be carried by image, action, recurrence, and voiced formula rather than flattening the myth into modern explanatory prose or a doctrinal essay. Oldness is a quality of remembered speech and ritual weight, not a license for decorative pseudo-archaism.

### Use lexical repetition deliberately

The active prose should reduce the frequency of `old`, retaining it where age, inheritance, ritual memory, or long duration matters most. Do not replace every removed instance with the same synonym; let torn banners, names, scars, posture, and remembered speech carry age through concrete detail.

The opening should also reduce its cluster of `fear` statements. Vaerun's hesitation is not generic dread. He knows that answering the cry and crossing the veil will require costly fidelity, and he wrongly assumes that he must be sufficient to measure what fidelity demands. He fears failing to become that measure. The later Shepherd-centered revelation corrects the premise: Vaerun was never the measure. Let reluctance, breath, trembling, silence, and the names beneath the banners embody this burden before narration explains it.

### Let the myth perform keeping

*Aru Va'en* should itself be a keeper of the Troth. As an entrusted mythic and liturgical witness, it should preserve memory, guard what has been entrusted, expose false possession, and call its hearers back beneath the Shepherd's judgment and mercy. This vocation does not mean that the myth possesses the Troth, becomes the measure of the Good, claims revelation or Scriptural authority, or stands beside Christ. The myth keeps only as it is itself kept and judged by the Shepherd.

### Require Scriptural review before final acceptance

No revised passage, sequence, or complete rewrite may receive final acceptance merely because it is narratively coherent or theologically attractive. Material changes remain provisional until a thorough Scriptural review has been documented. Before final acceptance, that review must:

1. identify the biblical passages relevant to each major Christological and theological claim;
2. distinguish quotation, paraphrase, exegesis, canonical synthesis, constructive theology, mythopoesis, and liturgical application;
3. preserve serious rival readings and state the inferential steps connecting Scripture to the myth;
4. test the Shepherd against the canonical witness to Christ's identity, lordship, incarnation, cross, resurrection, judgment, mercy, and reconciling work;
5. test every account of the Troth, keeping, violence, authority, redemption, and morning for whether it remains subordinate to and judged by Christ;
6. record unresolved tensions rather than concealing them; and
7. submit the resulting review and revision to the user for final theological and literary acceptance.

The review should be preserved as a dossier under `biblical/` before the revised myth is accepted as final.

## Disposition key

- **Preserve:** retain the scene's function and most of its imagery.
- **Revise:** retain the scene but alter its governing action, claim, or emphasis.
- **Replace:** preserve only the narrative need; rebuild the scene mechanics.
- **Resolve:** no prose change should be drafted until the named decision is made.

## Global constraints

Every accepted revision must preserve these controls:

1. Christ is the final measure; the Troth is not a second Logos or deity.
2. Violence remains real, but violence does not resolve the myth.
3. The Black Sun remains an intense exposure, accusation, and deformation.
4. The fallen remain responsible persons capable of return.
5. Fang does not confer guilt and fleece does not confer virtue.
6. The cry commands attention but does not bypass truth and discernment.
7. Authority assigns responsibility without certifying righteousness.
8. The Shepherd keeps the keepers; no office, circle, or ancestral body keeps alone.
9. Morning preserves wounds and truth; it does not reset the ordeal.
10. The communal greeting uses the settled three-part exchange.
11. The old liturgical register remains part of the myth's substance, not removable ornament.
12. The myth performs a keeper's vocation without possessing the Troth or becoming Scripture, revelation, or measure.
13. Final acceptance requires the documented Scriptural and Christological review defined above.
14. `Aruvan` is the only active spelling; `Aruvann` names nothing separate.
15. Repetition of `old` and `fear` must carry ritual or psychological development rather than ambient solemnity or explanation.
16. Before the hill the narrator is **a Vaerun**; only after the hill do the many call him **the Vaerun**. The later title is accrued stature, not a new office or proof of righteousness.
17. The wolves resist by least harm: hold before hitting; when holding fails, they may throw an immediate attacker back without drawing blood; they never maim or kill.
18. Treat Aruvan and the Living Lamb as two faces of one creaturely communion beneath the Shepherd: keeping and life kept, restored, gathered, and becoming companion.
19. The Shepherd is recognizably Christological from his first appearance but receives no opening superscription and is not treated as a hidden identity or final-act twist. **Vey's ending names his priority rather than unmasking him.**
