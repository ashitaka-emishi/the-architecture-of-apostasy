## The Fifth Keeping: Mourn

Where no companion could follow and no voice could answer for me, I was given a thing I have spoken only in this Keeping.

Not because it was hidden.

Because it was too large for ordinary speech.

I do not know whether the others saw it.

Some visions are not given to the worthy.

They enter at the breaking place, where breaking has made room.

I saw the hollow of stone beneath the torn banners, though no roof stood over it and no walls enclosed it.

I saw the stone seat.

Empty.

Not abandoned.

Behind it lay a wolf vast enough to shelter the hollow as mountains shelter a valley.

It did not crouch to spring.

It did not open its jaws to devour.

Its body had been gathered from keeping: memory beside memory, wound beside wound, vows borne with failure, responsibility received and passed on. No life within it had ceased to be itself.

Aruvan.

The long wolf made by keeping.

Not by conquest.

Not by hunger.

Not by an office preserving itself.

I saw the Hrovan I had known lay down what the hall had entrusted. Vaelun kept thresholds open. Vaerun crossed with those who could not cross alone. Gravane returned forgotten names to the wounded. Truvane took one another's weight.

I had thought Aruvan was the greatest body I would ever see.

Then it lowered its head.

Not to me.

To the one before it.

There stood the Shepherd.

A man.

Wounded.

Breathing.

His robe torn.

His human hands scarred and open.

The Black Sun had called grief the final name of the world. The Shepherd bore grief without making it final. Love had come first; only the beloved could be grieved.

Aruvan bowed lower.

The ribs that had enclosed generations opened without breaking. Names, memories, warnings, skills, debts, and unfinished responsibilities passed outward like breath returned to living lungs.

Nothing faithful was lost.

Nothing carried long remained possessed.

Through the ribs came the flock.

Not pale figures.

Not obedient shadows.

Living creatures, each in its own body. The lamb I had carried was among them and remained the lamb: wounded, breathing, choosing its steps. Others gathered near it without dissolving into it.

I saw then one communion under two forms.

The living flock moved through the open ribs toward the Shepherd.

He received them without closing his hands around them.

The vast shape became breath, warmth, and memory returned to those responsible to bear it. No ancestor or hidden hall remained behind the world.

The Shepherd remained.

Not by force.

Not by command.

By the fidelity no creature had made and no failure could exhaust.

The vision receded, drawing the hollow away.

The torn banners folded into darkness.

Someone was calling me back.

Not loudly.

Not by command.

By morning.

## Epilogue

When I opened my eyes, the hill remained.

Mud held the print of every body that had crossed it. Blood darkened the grass. The wounded lay where they had been carried. Harrow's body rested beneath the covering we had drawn over him. The air smelled of opened flesh, trampled roots, damp wool, and the first water moving again beneath the stones.

The Black Sun no longer held the east.

An ordinary sun rose there.

The White Moon faded at the other rim, its pale light not destroyed but gathered into morning.

The ground was this wounded world, still capable of bearing what had been promised to it.

Grass stood where it had not been torn away. Dew lay beside blood without confusing the two. Wolf, sheep, mud, milk, herbs, and running water returned to their own scents.

The lamb was still between my jaws.

I opened them.

It stepped down by its own strength.

The binding remained around its body. One wound still seeped where the struggle had opened it again. Its legs trembled, but it did not fall.

It looked first toward the Shepherd.

Then it waited.

The sheep whose eye he had opened stood a short distance away. It remained marked by what it had done and by what had been done to it.

The lamb went to it.

The sheep lowered its opened eye. The lamb stood beside it. Together they walked toward the Shepherd.

The flock began to follow at different distances.

Some came near.

Some remained beyond the reach of any fang.

Some had withdrawn before morning and did not return.

The Shepherd received those who came and held no offense against those who did not.

The veil thinned with the coming of the sun. Gravane crossed first.

With them, we counted the living.

We counted again.

We named the missing.

We tended the wounds that could be tended. Yarrow cleaned my side and left the opening to close in flesh's time. Murn's damaged ribs made every breath a rough labor. Sable's voice returned in fragments and broke when he tested it. Little Vey leaned his uninjured ear toward whoever spoke and missed some words on the torn side. Brindle rose beneath a back that would remain bent and painful.

Other hurts were tended and left unnamed.

Among the whole company, Gravane perceived something scars could not measure.

Vethra.

It was borne by every companion who had passed through the Keeping, wounded and unwounded, living and dead. It did not shine or take one shape. It made familiar flesh seem almost otherwise, but not with the wrongness the fallen had worn.

Coal beneath ash.

First light before fullness.

Fidelity considered and borne, not payment or proof.

Its measure belonged to the Shepherd.

The Gravane paused over only a few.

They came last to Harrow, named him among the dead, and covered him. Their hands lingered at his brow; the first light was borne even there, though no breath returned.

No answer came from beneath the cloth.

No answer was demanded.

When the dead were named, grief opened Ostra's stance until another mourner could stand beside her. Her scar remained at her ribs. Her three brothers did not return with morning.

Vethra was borne and left unclaimed.

After the missing had been counted, the wounded tended, and the dead covered, the defensive circle gave way to an open ring.

We made passages through it around the Shepherd. There was no head place and no seat from the hall.

Harrow's absence remained among us as an unfilled space.

We placed no food there.

Then the Hrovan and the Truvane came on foot through the opened veil.

They brought bread and water.

No banners came with them.

The Hrovan entered the ring, set the provision upon the ground, and stood beside it. Then he spoke:

> I sent you beneath a charge I had not understood.
>
> The stone seat entrusted me to answer.
>
> It did not make my answer faithful.

The gathering answered with silence.

I did not absolve him.

The Hrovan waited beside the bread and water.

The Shepherd came to the provision.

His hands were the same hands I had seen beneath the Black Sun.

Scarred.

Human.

Still marked with the lamb's blood beneath the washing of morning.

He received what the Hrovan had brought, gave thanks, broke the bread, and opened his hands.

The provision became common.

The Hrovan poured water first for the wounded. He drank only after another vessel had passed from his hands.

The Gravane spoke the names of the dead as they carried water to the living.

The Truvane carried bread and water through every passage. The wolf who had said, "Not this crossing," served among them.

The Vaelun changed places at the passages so each could eat.

I took bread across the line where the circle had first closed and carried it to the flock and those beginning return.

When each had received what could be received, I came back among my companions.

I did not preside.

The wounded lamb walked beside the returning sheep into the open ring. The flock approached without being herded. Some ate close to the Shepherd. Some took bread and withdrew. Fang and fleece shared hunger and remained themselves.

No lamb was killed for the meal.

Morning was not the Feast.

It was bread eaten in its promise.

Vethra remained nearly hidden as bread passed from one creature to another.

No seat, vessel, or portion was set for the Troth.

The meal quieted.

The lamb rested against the Shepherd. Its wounds remained bound. Its breath rose and fell beneath one scarred hand.

Little Vey came and sat beside me.

He did not draw the eye.

Only when my wounded side began to fail did he move beneath it, bearing enough of my weight that I did not stand alone.

We watched the Shepherd together.

Vey spoke once.

"Before the Troth, there was the Shepherd."

Before the vow was the cry.

Before grief was love.

We do not keep alone.

## Spelling and Pronunciation Guide

**Aru va'en** — AH-roo VAH-en

A keeping of the Troth; the response of the faithful at the calling.

**Aruvan** — ah-ROO-vahn

The long wolf made by keeping; the gathered body of faithful memory, responsibility, and practice across generations, relinquished beneath the Shepherd rather than preserved as an autonomous sacred body.

**Gravane** — grah-VAH-neh

Scar-bearers and rememberers; those who carry and press the old signs, return forgotten names to the wounded, and perceive Vethra fallibly.

**Hrovan** — HROH-vahn

The one who bears the hall; the lord or center-bearer entrusted with responsibility for the people. The office does not certify righteousness.

**Truvane** — troo-VAH-neh

The gathered faithful; those who have not been released from the Troth.

**Vaelun** — VAY-loon

Threshold-keepers; those set at the border between worlds.

**Vaerun** — VAY-roon

The office of crossing; the one bound to pass the veil with those who cannot cross alone.

**Vethra** — VETH-rah

First light beneath ash; the subtle testimony borne upon flesh through which fidelity has passed. It is not a wound, credential, payment, curse-mark, or institutional sign.

**Vey** — VAY

An old word: small, then ember, then the last light under ash.

**Ur va. Thar un. Veth ara.** — OOR VAH. THAHR OON. VETH AH-rah.

The old tongue of the calling; literal meaning lost, understood by trembling.
