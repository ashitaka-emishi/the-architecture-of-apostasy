## The Fourth Keeping: Eternal Dawn

We ran toward the hill in twilight.

Twelve beside me.

Twelve Vaelun.

No one spoke after we crossed the stones.

The veil closed behind us without sound.

The White Moon still hung at the edge of the world, thin and stricken, not yet gone from the sky.

Then the Black Sun rose against it.

It came without morning.

The twilight did not become dawn.

It became exposure.

One moment we were moving through the last gray of the borderlands. The next, every wound was visible. Every tooth. Every fly. Every smear of blood drying black in wool.

Black light from the east.

Pale light kept in the west.

Between them, the sky opened like a wound torn anew.

The Black Sun did not warm the world.

It opened it.

Every scent rose naked beneath it: blood in wool, terror in milk, fever under skin, rot sweetening at the edge of wounds, dung, wet iron, trampled grass, and the rank breath of creatures who had been made strange to themselves.

Nothing could hide its smell.

Not the lamb.

Not those who had harmed it.

Not us.

Exposure was not yet truth.

The Black Sun showed each fragment alone and called the fragment whole.

The hill stood before us as though it had been waiting inside the wound.

The Shepherd saw us coming.

He did not smile.

He did not thank us.

He looked at us as one looks at rain after drought: with grief and recognition.

Behind him, the White Moon sank lower but did not vanish.

Above him, the Black Sun climbed.

We formed the circle around him.

The first mercy was knowing where to stand.

The lamb cried once.

Then the assault came up the hill.

It broke upon us like weather.

The first body struck Harrow and tore his shoulder. Brindle and Thorn caught the assailant between them, shoulder against flank, and turned its teeth away. They held until it ceased lunging, then opened a path down the hill and let it go.

No fang entered it.

No claw opened it.

Harrow bled.

That was how the cost began.

At the center, the Shepherd began to call.

Not to us alone.

He called into the flock below, name after name, as though no scream, false voice, or Black Sun could make him forget whom he had come to gather.

While he called, he worked.

He pressed one scarred hand beneath the lamb's breast and waited through each broken breath. With the other he tore strips from his robe, washed mud from the wounds with water from a fold of cloth, and bound what had been opened. He did not hurry the creature past its pain. He did not wait for it to prove a will to live.

Blood slowed beneath his hands.

Breath ceased its drowning.

Torn flesh drew by degrees toward torn flesh.

The lamb remained wounded.

Still the Shepherd called.

Its near ear moved beneath his voice.

Later, with an effort that shook its neck, its head turned toward him.

It did not stand.

It did not answer.

It began to remember what it was.

Around him, we made room between bodies as he made room within them.

Rook sent Fen into the falling press. Keld and Murn bore a wolf-shaped assailant to earth. When its limbs stopped striking, they rolled away and left the hill open beneath it.

Claws entered us.

Teeth found us.

Fever passed across torn skin. The assailants screamed with voices almost like ours. Some wore the shoulders of wolves, some the faces of sheep, some a likeness that shifted with the eye that watched it. Some we sheltered struck when touched. Some we held collapsed when their rage had passed.

An assailant broke Keld's hold and came for the lamb. I met it with my shoulder and threw it down the slope. It rolled, found its feet, and stopped beyond the reach of my body.

Still I followed.

One step.

Then another.

The back of its neck opened before my sight. I wanted the ending my teeth could give.

Thorn struck my ribs from the side and set his body across my path.

I turned on him.

At the center, the Shepherd's face changed.

He did not command me back.

His grief grew.

His grief had room for every creature on the hill without calling the harm good.

The lamb felt it and shuddered beneath his hands.

In that grief I saw protection changing its face within me. The Black Sun offered the first blow as warrant for the next.

I returned to my place.

Thorn returned with me.

The Shepherd bent again over the lamb.

Then the fallen came among the assailants.

Not apart from the assault.

Within it.

They wore voices we loved.

A brother's laugh, almost right.

A mother's call, almost tender.

The face of a dead companion held a breath too long, as though something had learned the shape but not the sorrow.

Harrow's laugh came from a mouth that did not smell of Harrow.

Sable's song came from a throat without Sable's trembling in it.

Little Vey's face appeared below us without the warm scent of ash and rain that belonged to him.

One wore my eyes and smelled of a den I had never entered.

Each likeness was wrong by less than sight could name.

Not enough to pronounce a verdict.

Enough to cause the blood to hesitate.

They did not merely lie.

They accused with what was true.

One called Harrow steadfast and showed him every time steadfastness had become refusal to receive help.

One sang with Sable's hidden voice and showed him every silence in which he had withheld the song another needed.

One wore Murn's younger face and told him that age had made every burden he accepted into a burden placed upon others.

One looked through my eyes and showed me how quickly I had desired pursuit.

The acts were not invented.

The verdict was.

This is all you are.

This is all you have ever been.

This is all you can become.

The four curses passed over the hill like shadows over water.

Harrow's fang drew inward until the point rested against his own throat. Far below, a dry film crossed the eye of a sheep beneath the press. Ash's bloodied paw became smooth beneath him, clean of scent and weight. Sable's breath struck a closure behind his teeth, and the song shook there without passage.

The marks crossed faithful and fallen alike.

At the center, the Shepherd did not sort clean bodies from deformed ones.

He sorted within them.

He laid two fingers against Harrow's jaw and drew the fang away from his throat, turning its point toward the open boundary where another blow might come. He took Ash's smooth paw in his scarred hand and pressed it into mud, wool, pulse, and the weight of another creature needing help. He touched Sable beneath the jaw until the trapped breath passed his teeth as one broken note.

He cut nothing away.

He returned each part within the circle to its body and each body toward relation beneath him.

Then he called again.

Far down the hill, the sheep beneath the ash-dry film heard.

The Shepherd had spoken a name I did not know. In the press below, one ear turned toward him. The sheep's body followed by less than a paw's width.

From the sheep came a cry too soft for the rest of us to hear.

Little Vey heard it.

Then its scent reached him: fever, ash, harm done and received, and beneath them earth opening under rain.

Vey smelled remembrance.

He said nothing. He slipped behind the bodies holding the lower edge, crossed beneath Brindle's bent stance, and sought me. At my wounded side, he set his head below my shoulder and turned toward the movement he had found.

His weight steadied mine.

I followed his attention. Rook followed mine. Then the others saw.

The sheep below us had been driving a smaller creature uphill beneath its chest. At another command from the shifting host, it lowered its head to strike.

The Shepherd called its name again.

The sheep stopped. Its body shook. The command came again from behind it.

The sheep turned sideways instead.

It took the blow meant for the smaller creature, then lifted one foreleg and made an opening beneath itself.

The smaller creature crawled through.

Our circle opened.

Fen and Brindle drew apart while Keld and Thorn held the press.

It passed first.

The sheep followed.

No fang closed. Its passage erased no harm, and no wounded creature was asked to trust it.

The Shepherd made room beside the lamb and received the smaller creature. Then he turned to the sheep.

The ash-colored film over its eye had hardened like wax. He worked his thumb beneath its edge and drew it away, opening the eye beneath it.

The sheep flinched at the first depth of the world.

The first thing it saw clearly was the wolves holding the passage.

It saw our teeth and saw that they did not close. It saw our bodies holding back harm while leaving a way through. Its breath changed. Fang had returned to its proper place.

Then it looked beyond us to the Shepherd for whom the passage had been held.

The hill saw with it. Some began to turn. Some withdrew. Some climbed harder, as though an opened way were an accusation they could not bear.

Return had become visible.

It had not become easy.

Little Vey was moving back toward his place when a flailing claw caught one ear and tore it along the outer fold.

He fell, rose, and leaned toward his better side to hear.

Yarrow bound the ear while Vey pressed close beneath my shoulder again.

The assault tightened.

Harrow took a second wound beneath the first. Brindle and Rook carried him inward before the next body fell. Yarrow pressed herbs and cloth into the opening. Murn lay along Harrow's back to keep him warm. The circle closed against the blow and opened to their need.

Harrow died there.

We covered him enough to keep mud from his face, and his place among the living remained empty.

Murn rose from beside him with ribs damaged and breath roughened. He was still Murn. Sable's voice broke when he tried to sing, but a broken song passed where silence had sealed him. Brindle's back bent beneath a falling body and did not straighten. Blood ran along my side from rib toward shoulder.

Still the Shepherd called.

Still he tended.

The lamb's breathing strengthened. Its head no longer fell away from his voice. When a body broke against the inner ring and jarred the Shepherd to one knee, the lamb's bound wound opened and blood warmed his palm again.

The Shepherd gathered it closer.

His grief deepened.

We felt its room at our backs. The circle yielded beneath the blow, widened around the fallen, and held.

Under the Black Sun, the ordeal seemed to outlast every hour by which creatures measure night. Yet the White Moon remained at the edge, thin as mercy and just as easily overlooked.

Then came the wish.

Small.

Shameful.

Merciful, it almost seemed.

That the lamb would stop crying.

Not die. Only fall silent, and end the tearing inside me.

The Black Sun found the truth in that wish.

It showed me every cry I had answered because I wanted to be the one who answered. It showed me the stone seat I claimed not to desire. It showed me my teeth at the boundary and the pleasure of one step taken too far.

Then it pronounced the lie.

You have never wanted the cry.

You have wanted only to be named faithful for hearing it.

My paw lost the smell of blood.

Mud became smooth beneath it.

My mouth closed gently around the lamb's sound.

Not biting.

Closing.

The hill became clean.

No wool.

No milk.

No blood.

No small life breathing against the Shepherd's torn robe.

I began to fall inward, into a silence warm as a den and without any cry.

At my side, Little Vey shifted his weight beneath mine.

He did not tell me what I was becoming.

He simply remained where I could feel him.

Across the clean emptiness, the Shepherd's grief met me.

Not accusation.

Not permission.

Grief for the cry I wanted ended, and for the creature in me closing its mouth around it.

The lamb turned its wounded head toward his voice.

One breath entered it.

Then another.

The world regained its smell.

Blood.

Mud.

Wool.

Vey's torn ear bound beside me.

My falsely clean paw sank into the earth beneath his weight. The trapped cry passed my teeth as breath.

I opened a passage.

I held.

The circle had lost Harrow.

His place remained empty.

Beneath the Shepherd, the living held one another.

The Black Sun pressed lower.

The hill filled with cries: the wounded, the returning, those enraged by return, and those who had forgotten which cry was theirs. The Shepherd answered with names.

No blow broke the Black Sun.

No death purchased morning.

Beneath his calling, the Black Sun's final sentence began to crack.

The assault did not.

It came once more with all the weight left in it.

Fen sank beneath Rook. Ash and Yarrow carried the wounded inward. Thorn threw back a body, then returned to the opening. Sable gave one torn note, and the circle shifted around it.

Brindle's legs folded.

The inner ring opened around him.

Through that opening an assailant reached the Shepherd.

I entered between them.

The strike opened my side.

I threw the body only far enough for Thorn and Keld to take its movement from it. Then my forelegs failed.

I did not rise.

The ground received me.

At the center, the Shepherd lowered the lamb beside his knee without releasing it from his care.

One of the host struck him, its mouth closed around its own cry.

He opened both hands and gave ground beneath its weight.

He did not throw it back.

The lamb stood.

Its legs shook.

Blood still marked the binding around its body.

It looked toward the Shepherd.

Then the press of bodies cut the few steps between the lamb and the Shepherd.

I lay across that narrow crossing, my jaws open with exhaustion.

The lamb came to me.

No one placed it in my mouth.

It stepped between my teeth and chose the shelter there.

I closed upon wool and nothing more.

Fang became cradle.

With the strength left in me, I carried the lamb beyond the falling bodies and into the room the circle had made.

One step.

Then none.

The lamb remained warm between my jaws.

Darkness received me.
