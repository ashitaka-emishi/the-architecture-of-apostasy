# The Book of Wolves
## Letters Concerning the Keeping of the Troth

## Status

This is the active proposed design for the Book of Wolves workstream. It incorporates the Book of Wolves development summary supplied by the user and supersedes the looser earlier title-and-sequence proposals in this folder.

The work remains mythopoetic correspondence, not Scripture, not a systematic theology, and not ancient pseudepigraphy. It may echo New Testament epistolary form without claiming canonical status or apostolic provenance.

## Governing Form

The preferred title is:

> **The Book of Wolves**  
> *Letters Concerning the Keeping of the Troth*

The collection is composed primarily of letters among wolves. From outside the mythology they may be described as mythopoetic epistles. Within the mythology they should remain letters: concrete correspondence about particular burdens, recipients, histories, failures, and forms of keeping.

The letters are personal without being merely private. Andren may write to Harrow, but the question raised through Harrow concerns all wolves tempted to identify vow with Troth. A letter may also be addressed to an heir, descendant, student, successor, community, or company of wolves who have inherited a companion's memory or burden.

## Wolves Beneath the Shepherd

These are letters between wolves, not men represented metaphorically as wolves. Lupine identity is real within the myth and must preserve the distinction between wolves and sheep.

Wolves are associated with fang, strength, watchfulness, endurance, guardianship, territoriality, violence, resistance, remaining where others cannot remain, and the danger of turning protection into possession.

The governing question of the book is:

> **How shall a wolf live beneath the Shepherd?**

The answer is not that wolves become sheep. The wolf remains wolf. Redemption does not erase creaturely distinction; it reorders wolfly capacity beneath Christ.

> The fang remains a fang.  
> The watch remains a watch.  
> Strength remains strength.  
> But none of them belong finally to the wolf.

The separation between wolf and sheep must never become dualism. Wolves are not sheep. Wolves are not shepherds. Yet wolves too belong to the Shepherd. Difference of vocation does not imply difference of Lord.

## Christological Ordering

The Shepherd has dominion over pasture, flock, wolf, wilderness, keeper, and all created things. The Book of Wolves must not let the wolf archetype become a rival spiritual order.

The implicit command over the wolf is:

> Your fang is mine.  
> Your watch is mine.  
> Your strength is mine.  
> Your keeping is mine.  
> And you are mine.

The Shepherd is therefore the measure of the book's treatment of strength, fear, sight, office, vow, tradition, mercy, suffering, and keeping.

## The Troth

The letters explicate the Troth through tested situations rather than by constructing a complete doctrinal system.

Within this book's working grammar:

> **The Troth is the faithfulness that precedes and exceeds every vow, office, institution, keeper, and particular act of keeping.**

Therefore the vow is not the Troth, the office is not the Troth, the guardian is not the Troth, the community is not the Troth, the inherited structure is not the Troth, and the keeper is not the Troth. All may serve the Troth. None may replace it.

A central principle:

> **Nothing that keeps is the thing being kept.**

Keeping is contingent and creaturely. The Troth precedes the keeper and remains when particular forms of keeping end.

## Unclaimed Virtue

The Book of Wolves functions as a narrative and epistolary exposition of the Doctrine of Unclaimed Virtue. The doctrine should recur in many forms rather than appear only once.

The common temptation is:

> Because something good, difficult, painful, discerning, or faithful passed through me, I may now possess the virtue associated with it.

The letters repeatedly deny that inference:

- I kept my vow, therefore I am faithful.
- I suffered, therefore I am righteous.
- I guarded them, therefore they are mine.
- I saw the danger, therefore I see more truly than others.
- I endured, therefore I am good.
- I possess strength, therefore my use of strength is justified.
- I bear the office, therefore I am the keeper.

The correction:

> **What is entrusted to a wolf does not become the wolf's possession merely because he carried it faithfully.**

Virtue cannot be converted into ownership.

## Andren and Vaerun

For the Book of Wolves, the personal name behind the Vaerun is **Andren**.

This is a Book-of-Troth literary decision and does not change the accepted *Aru Va'en* narration, where the narrator's personal name remains unstated. The relation is:

**Andrew -> Andren -> Vaerun**

Andrew is the author outside the myth. Andren is the author refracted into the myth. Vaerun is the office carried by Andren.

Andren must remain subject to the same critique he applies to other wolves. He does not stand outside the book as an unquestioned teacher. The book eventually turns his own teaching against him.

Andren should not routinely sign as **the Vaerun**. That would risk making office into identity. His authority arises from relationship and entrusted responsibility, not possession of office. The precise recurring signature remains open.

## Little Vey's Frame

Little Vey should write the prologue near the end of his life. He preserves the correspondence many years after much of it was written and may be the one who gives the collection its title.

The prologue should establish that these were letters, not originally written as Scripture or doctrine; that some recipients have died; that some letters address heirs or successors; that the letters came to be preserved together; that Andren bore the office of Vaerun; and that wolves remain beneath the Shepherd.

Little Vey should never receive an instructional letter from Andren. He is not Andren's pupil. Their relationship is more reciprocal and less hierarchical, which makes Little Vey uniquely suited to keep Andren when the Vaerun is endangered by his office.

Little Vey's smallness is not something Andren teaches him to transcend.

> **Little Vey never requires greatness in order to keep.**

## Name and Office

Because Little Vey writes the prologue, the reader may know from the beginning that the Vaerun was named Andren. The mystery is not "Who was the Vaerun?" but "Why does Little Vey insist on calling him Andren?"

Little Vey does not merely reveal Andren's name.

> **He gives Andren his name back.**

The final letter becomes an act of remembering Andren out of his identification with office.

## Twelve-Letter Form

The current intended structure is:

1. **Prologue by Little Vey** - written near the end of his life; explains preservation of the letters; frames wolves as distinct from sheep yet beneath the Shepherd.
2. **Harrow - *The Vow*: Concerning Oaths and the Troth** - Andren warns Harrow that the Troth is not the vow; relinquishing a vow may become the final act of keeping when fidelity to the vow would betray what the vow was meant to serve.
3. **Sable - *The Song*: Concerning Gift and Identity** - gift must remain gift; what passes through a wolf does not become his possession.
4. **Murn - *The Last Winter*: Concerning Mortality and Relinquishment** - age, diminishing strength, usefulness, death, and the passage of keeping into other paws.
5. **Brindle - *The Hidden Good*: Concerning Unclaimed Virtue** - goodness performed through a wolf does not prove that the wolf possesses goodness.
6. **Keld - *The Silence*: Concerning Presence and Withdrawal** - silence may be attentiveness, restraint, reverence, or depth; it may also become concealment, refusal, or abandonment.
7. **Ash - *The Fear*: Concerning Courage** - courage is not fearlessness; faithfulness may consist in answering while fear remains.
8. **Rook - *The Watch*: Concerning Discernment and Suspicion** - watchfulness corrupts when vigilance hardens into suspicion and fear mistakes itself for sight.
9. **Yarrow - *The Mercy*: Concerning the Fallen and Their Return** - mercy does not erase wounds or declare the guilty innocent; even for the fallen, the Troth remains.
10. **Thorn - *The Fang*: Concerning Strength and Guardianship** - strength is not rejected, but neither is it self-justifying; protection must not become domination.
11. **Ostra - *The Wound*: Concerning Grief and Authority** - suffering is real and must not be minimized, but the wound may teach; it cannot crown.
12. **Fen - *The Sinking Ground*: Concerning Remaining** - remaining faithful is not the same as preserving every inherited form; sometimes faithfulness consists in remaining with one another while the old ground disappears.
13. **Andren - *The Office*: Concerning the Vaerun** - written by Little Vey to Andren; the direction of correspondence reverses, and Vey applies Andren's teaching to Andren himself.

The numbered list includes the prologue plus twelve letters. The eleventh Andren-authored letter is Fen; the twelfth letter is Little Vey's corrective letter to Andren.

## Chiastic Structure

The twelve letters form a broad six-pair chiasm:

| Pair | Letters | Distinction | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Harrow / Andren | Vow / Office | Do not become the thing entrusted to you. |
| B | Sable / Fen | Gift / Remaining | What must actually remain when gifts, forms, traditions, and structures change? |
| C | Murn / Ostra | Mortality / Wound | Neither death nor suffering confers possession of virtue. |
| D | Brindle / Thorn | Hidden Good / Fang | Neither goodness nor power becomes righteousness because a wolf bears it. |
| E | Keld / Yarrow | Silence / Mercy | Keeping requires discerning when not to act and when another's cry cannot be left unanswered. |
| F | Ash / Rook | Fear / Sight | What do we fear, and what do we truly see? |

The book opens with Andren correcting Harrow:

> **Harrow, you are not your vow.**

It ends with Little Vey correcting Andren:

> **Andren, you are not the Vaerun.**

Little Vey does not introduce a new doctrine. He remembers Andren's own doctrine back to him.

## Generational Dimension

The book should span enough time for memory, inheritance, tradition, and reputation to become theological problems. Some letters may be written directly to living companions, shortly before death, after death, to descendants, to heirs of a duty, to successors in a watch, or to wolves living under an ancestor's reputation.

The major danger is:

> **The dead can become offices too.**

Legend can harden into unquestionable model. To keep the fathers faithfully, heirs must sometimes refuse to imitate them.

The Troth remains. The historical form of a previous keeping may not.

## Tradition and the Keeping Between

The generational letters, especially Fen's letter, naturally connect to **The Keeping Between**.

The proper inherited question is not:

> How do we keep everything our predecessors built?

It is:

> **What were they actually trying to keep?**

The Troth remains. This does not imply that every structure must remain. A keeper may be called to preserve something; a keeper may also be called to recognize that an inherited form is passing.

## What the Book Is Not

The Book of Wolves should not attempt to become a complete systematic theology of Christology, ecclesiology, soteriology, eschatology, sacramental theology, or metaphysics.

It is a practical and mythopoetic examination of keeping. Doctrine emerges through oath, gift, mortality, hidden goodness, silence, fear, discernment, mercy, strength, suffering, tradition, and office.

The reader should learn the doctrines by watching them tested.

## Central Wolf Problem

The wolf possesses dangerous capacities. The error would be to resolve that danger by declaring those capacities evil in themselves.

The Book strips away the wolf's claims without destroying the underlying gifts:

> I can guard, therefore I should rule.  
> I can see danger, therefore I understand everything.  
> I am strong, therefore my judgment is justified.  
> I endured, therefore I am righteous.  
> They depended upon me, therefore they belong to me.  
> I was entrusted with the office, therefore the office is mine.

The wolf is not saved by becoming harmless.

> **The wolf is saved when even his fang answers to the Shepherd.**

>

>

>
> **Nothing faithful keeps alone.**

## Final Movement

The collection begins with old Little Vey preserving Andren's letters and ends with the earlier Little Vey preserving Andren himself.

Old Vey keeps the Book. Younger Vey keeps Andren.

And through both acts:

> **The Troth remains.**
