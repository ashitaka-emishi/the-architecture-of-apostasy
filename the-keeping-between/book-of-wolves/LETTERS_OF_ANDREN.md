# Letters of Andren — Design Constraints

## Why letters

Letters permit a distinctly apostolic authority: witness, relationship, correction, exhortation, memory, and weakness rather than impersonal decree.

## Standard shape

A typical letter may contain:

1. **Greeting** — relational, not status-display.
2. **Shared memory** — a concrete scene from the Keeping or later fellowship.
3. **Report/problem** — “word has come to me…” or a question from the recipient.
4. **Self-implication** — Andren locates the danger in himself as well.
5. **Instruction** — Troth teaching applied to the case.
6. **Christological test** — how the teaching answers to the Shepherd/Christ.
7. **Practice** — what the recipient/community should actually do.
8. **Blessing/greeting** — often a seed of later liturgy.

## Required anti-hagiographic scene

At least one preserved letter should record Andren being corrected by another companion. A strong pattern is:

> You have made your certainty a throne.

Andren's initial anger followed by rereading and confession can embody Unclaimed Virtue rather than merely teach it.

The recovered *Letter of Little Vey to the Vaerun* supplies the source of the corrective letter. Its active working revision is `book-of-wolves/letters/LITTLE_VEY_TO_VAERUN.md`. What remains to be designed is Andren's reception, response, and any preserved confession.

## Established correspondence from Little Vey

Little Vey survives the Keeping and later writes to Andren as a living, embodied companion. His letter concerns stature, fame, hidden thrones, the insight of the small, and the keeper's need to be kept. Its governing challenge is the movement from **a Vaerun**, one bearer of the crossing vocation before the hill, to **the Vaerun**, the singular public stature created by the many afterward. Vey calls him to remember where the companions stood, when only they knew his name, where the Shepherd stood, and the Troth that kept none of them alone. Its placement and Andren's response remain open.

## Historical continuity problem

The frozen myth kills or removes multiple companions. The new peaceful-resistance/redemption direction may change those fates. Little Vey is the established exception: he survives and can participate in post-event correspondence. Do not draft a post-event letter to another companion until the narrative chronology permits it, unless the letter is explicitly placed before the final ordeal or presented as a remembered/unsent letter.
